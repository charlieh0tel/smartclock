# 58503A 3704 against Z3805A 3543B

What the 58503A's image, `third_party/firmware/58503a-3704.bin`
(revision 3704; the bench 58503A runs 3704-C), has that the Z3805A's,
`z3805a-3543b.bin`, the one image whose every function is named
(`third_party/firmware/z3805a-3543b/names.tsv`) and written by hand
(`third_party/firmware/z3805a-3543b/rewritten/`), does not, and the
reverse.  Addresses are given as Z3805A → 58503A.  `revisions.md`
compares 3704 with the 58503A's earlier 3633; much of what 3704 adds
over the Z3805A it adds over 3633 too, and is described there.

"The port" and the sections after it are the 58503A's hand-written C:
how it was made and checked, and where it does otherwise than the
Z3805A's.

## Method

`tools/firmware/image_diff.py` matches every function of the Z3805A's
export (the `.conventions.jsonl` sidecar, 2041 functions, the
installer's among them) to the other image by bytes: exactly; with
every address and displacement that leaves the body masked; by the same
instructions with other immediates and displacements ("fuzzy"); by the
SCPI handler of the same path; by the most alike instructions between
the counterparts of its matched neighbors; by the counterparts of the
functions it calls; and last by the address the matched callers' calls
name in its place.  Code the names do not cover is found and matched
the same way, as "pieces".  The 58503A's functions are found from the
matches, its vector tables, prologues after a return, calls, pointers
to the end of a known body and clean decoding on from one; those
nothing matched are "new".  The tool's docstring has the details and
its thresholds.

    python3 -I tools/firmware/image_diff.py \
        --functions third_party/firmware/z3805a-3543b/decompiled/z3805a-3543b.conventions.jsonl \
        --names third_party/firmware/z3805a-3543b/names.tsv \
        --modules third_party/firmware/z3805a-3543b/modules.tsv \
        --handlers third_party/firmware/z3805a-3543b/handlers.txt \
        third_party/firmware/58503a-3704/handlers.txt --installer \
        third_party/firmware/z3805a-3543b.bin third_party/firmware/58503a-3704.bin \
        > docs/firmware/diff/58503a-3704-vs-z3805a-3543b.tsv

`make image-diff` runs it and rewrites this document's figures from its
output (`tools/firmware/diff_figures.py`): the counts below, the share
carried, the Z3805A's hand-written C by match, the labels not placed and
the per-subject counts of "What changed in what both have"; and it
re-seeds the 58503A's `names.tsv` and `modules.tsv` from the same
matches (`tools/firmware/seed_names.py`, `make seed-names`; the names
file's header gives the rules and their exceptions).  `make
check-image-diff` fails while any is stale, as it is after the Z3805A's
names, modules or export change.  The lists of files in the prose are
not checked.

Its output is [`diff/58503a-3704-vs-z3805a-3543b.tsv`](diff/58503a-3704-vs-z3805a-3543b.tsv):
one row for each function, piece, typed label and new function, with
how it was matched, both addresses and sizes, the similarity (difflib
ratio of the instructions with numbers masked) and, for a changed one,
the instruction diff in brief, its calls and its strings named through
the matches.  Run against itself the Z3805A's functions and pieces come
out wholly identical, with nothing new; only typed labels no
instruction names, such as short strings inside a table, are not
placed.

- *Identical* is the same bytes, wherever they are; *relocated* the
  same with every ROM and RAM address and every outward displacement
  masked.  Both carry over as they stand.
- A *changed* row at similarity 1.000 has the same instructions with
  other immediates or displacements: almost always a field that moved
  in a structure, or another constant.  Below 0.5, and for a match by
  `callees` or `references`, the pairing is a lead, not a finding: a
  `references` row marked "replaced" is the routine the same callers
  call in its place.  One is known wrong: `save_calibration_block`
  (`0x30c60`), which writes the EEPROM's `0x400080`, is paired with
  `0x30c6c`, which writes `0x400000`; 3704's writer of `0x400080` is
  `0x30b24`, listed new, and `0x30c6c` is the counterpart of
  `save_parameter_record` (`0x30dea`), which the tool pairs with
  `0x30da4`.
- A new function is put in the module of the matched function before
  it, which places the front panel's code under `gps/oncore`; the
  sections below group by subject instead.
- The names file lists each new function as an `entry` row, which
  `SmartClockSetup.java` makes an unnamed function at (`README.md`,
  "Decompiling with Ghidra"), with the 8 a bare import's analysis
  finds called that image_diff does not (`seed_names.py`'s
  `CALLED_UNLISTED`); a function named by hand is a function row.
- image_diff takes no start that cannot be a function's: one whose
  bytes are text to a NUL, or a table of pointers (its first long zero
  or an address); one inside a ROM table it places; and one inside
  another function's instructions.  The rule rejects 19 starts in the
  58503A, most from pointers image_diff read to odd addresses: 6 inside
  a string, 2 inside typed data (`0x2238a`, the health monitor's
  channel table; `0x413ca`), 7 inside an instruction, 2 tables of
  pointers (`0x29788`, `0x5116c`), the `rts` ending `0x2d946`, and
  `0x4fff0`, 2 bytes into `oncore_ai_decode`, which is paired at
  `0x4ffee`.  It drops the Z3805A's pieces inside a string, a table or
  a named function (154 to 92 in the primary).  With the starts gone
  the handler of `:SYSTem:COMMunicate?` (`0x2fefe`) pairs with the
  Z3805A's.
- Rows of `unassigned/...` are routines `modules.tsv` leaves out: the
  assembly, the pForth kernel, the boot code's exceptions; the part
  after the slash is the sidecar's family or convention.

## What the Z3805A has and the 58503A does not

- **The SCI driver** (`devices/sci`, 15, and `sci_init`, `sci_lock`,
  `sci_unlock`, `sci_write`, their ISR `primary_user_64` `0x1f988`,
  `sci_settings_preset` `0x30ed4`).  In the Z3805A the SCI carries
  only the ES message: the host port is DUART channel B, device 0 of
  the I/O table (`0x5171a`, `drtb_read` and `drtb_write`), which
  `scpi_port_open` opens (`0x1dee6`), and `sci_init` sets the flag
  (`0x10329c`, at `0x1ffc8`) under which `sci_write` (`0x20152`)
  drops text.  The 58503A writes no
  SCI register and has none of the driver's strings (`sciR`, `sciW`,
  `SCI isr ...`).  Its SCPI ports are served by the DUART channel B
  driver, which both images have (`drtR`, `drtW`).
- **The ES message and the time code's output compares**: `es_build`
  (`0x13a6a`), `timecode_schedule` (`0x13fa6`), `es_schedule`
  (`0x1406a`), the GPT interrupt `primary_user_87` (`0x140e4`) and
  `gpt_input_capture_2_interrupt` (`0x45cc0`).  These are the Z3805A's
  only users of the GPT's TCNT, TIC1, TOC3, TOC4, TMSK1 and TFLG1
  (`0xfff90a` to `0xfff922`, MC68331UM D.2); the 58503A touches none
  of them.  Its `timecode_update` (`0x13c50` →
  `0x13da8`) no longer builds the ES message nor reads the clock's
  date and time, and `pps_interrupt` (`0x12db6` → `0x12f5e`) calls it.
- **One ADC routine per channel**: `read_adc_temp`, `_5v`, `_oven`,
  `_doven`, `_p15v`, `_m15v`, `_osc_curr`, `_ant_curr` (`0x1f308` to
  `0x1f4ba`), the console words `adc_temp` to `adc_ant_curr` and
  `pr_adc_avg` (`0x1b008` to `0x1b0f6`) and `hmon_check_supplies`;
  3704's `monitors_reset` (`0x23dc2`) clears the monitors through
  `adc_monitors_reset` (`0x21cfc`), with no supplies' step.  3704
  reads every channel through one routine
  with the channel as argument (`0x21ca2`) and checks them from a
  table of descriptors (below).  `pforth/models.md` lists the words.
- **`:SAMPle`**, 48 paths: the 22 handlers of
  `scpi_sample.c` and `sample_action_print` (`0x43426`).
- **`:SOURce:ROSCillator:HOLDover:DURation:STATus:THReshold`** with its
  `ALARm1`, `ALARm3` and `EXCeeded` forms, and `:LED:TMHValid?`.
- **Port GP and the DUART output port as the Z3805A uses them**:
  `gp_port_select` (`0x1d572`, PORTGP bits 4 to 2, which 3633 still
  writes, `revisions.md`), `duart_op0_set` and `duart_op1_set`
  (`0x1dfe6`, `0x1e02c`, the DUART's set and reset output-port
  commands `0x20001d`, `0x20001f`).  3704 has one routine for any of
  the output port's bits, `duart_output_bit_set` (`0x1de4e`), and one
  that reads the input port, `duart_input_port_read` (`0x1de46`, IP at
  the OPCR's address, `0x20001b`), neither called; its boot code writes
  the OPCR, which the Z3805A's never does.
- **The lamps as the Z3805A drives them**: `leds_update` (`0x464de`)
  and `status_summary_update` (`0x46470`), which `pllp_task` calls at
  the end of each pass; 3704's `pllp_task` (`0x47716`) calls neither.
  It drives the latch at `0x300000` through three new setters,
  `latch_bits_set`, `latch_bits_write` and `latch_lamps_write`
  (`0x136c0`, `0x13702`, `0x1373c`: set or clear bits, a field under
  a mask, the low three bits), each writing `diag_tmode_data`
  (`0x101e77`) in place of its shadow while `diag_tmode_state`
  (`0x101e76`, `:DIAGnostic:TMODe:STATe`) is set.
- `scpi_task` (`0x2a04a`), `status_transitions_init` (`0x2f40c`),
  the power self-tests `selftest_power`, `supplies_test`,
  `selftest_power_with_lamp`
  (`0x17f2c`, `0x18546`, `0x18d96`), `gps_parse_options` (`0x4f8b6`),
  `gps_script_lookup` (`0x4be04`), `gps_receiver_status_reset`
  (`0x4b112`): each has a successor of another shape, below;
  `scpi_task`, the three power self-tests and `gps_script_lookup` are
  carried under their names to theirs ("Names and the seeding").

## What the 58503A adds

- **A front panel.**  A task `fpnl` (entry `0x48018`), which
  `root_task` (`0x1304a` → `0x1321e`) creates where the Z3805A creates
  none, drives Option 001's display and keypad, a device on the QSPI
  framed by bits 6 and 7 of the latch at `0x300000` (`0x13702`,
  `0x136c0`; `outputs.md`): 15 routines from `0x5274e` to `0x5395c`,
  2.6 KB, among them the key handler with `NO ALARM` and `ALRM CLEARED`
  (`0x5362e`, the one `revisions.md` traces back to 3633) and a
  self-test that reports `Passed` or `Failed` (`0x537ea`).  What the
  code sends it and reads back is "The front panel", under "Hardware"
  below.
- **The health monitor by table** (`0x21e3e` to `0x222f2`, 17
  routines, and the descriptors at `0x2238a`): one channel per call,
  round robin, with one message format, `%s is out of tolerance,
  value: %.2f`; `revisions.md`, "Health monitor", has its channels and
  limits.  `hmon_task` (`0x247d4` → `0x23b88`) calls it in place of
  `hmon_check_supplies`.  The power-on check `0x18046` (`POWER: ` and
  the descriptor's name) takes the place of the Z3805A's power
  self-tests.
- **SCPI on more than one port.**  The port layer gains a dispatcher
  per operation, each a switch on the port number with a case for port
  2 alone (`0x290ce` to `0x29246`), and a new SCPI task body (`0x294cc`,
  calling `0x293c2`), which `scpi_task_create` (`0x29fce` → `0x294f8`)
  starts where the Z3805A's starts `scpi_task`.  `:SYSTem:COMMunicate` names
  the ports `ALARm`, `AUX`, `BOOT`, `CRAFt`, `DRTB`, `LOCal`, `PFORth`,
  `REMote`, `SCI` and `TOD` beside `SERial1` and `SERial2`.  The
  routine among them that tests for 2, `0x291f6`, is not a port's: its
  one caller passes 2, and it computes the alarm, which also drives
  bit 5 of PORTF0 and FPGA field 6 through `0x40828` ("The alarm
  outputs", under "Hardware" below).
- **The external 1 PPS reference.**  Status and log lines for it
  (`EXT reference valid at ...`, `: switched to Ext 1PPS reference`,
  `0x31ac4`), the 22-byte reference records and their states
  (`revisions.md`, "Reference state"), the console's `force_ext_1pps`,
  and `:DIAGnostic:REFerence:EXTernal`.  The two new routines that
  read a port until it is steady (`0x234ae`, `0x234f8`) are not the
  reference's: nothing calls them ("Reads until steady", under
  "Hardware" below).
- **Eight-channel engines.**  The six- or eight-channel selection, the
  `@@Cj` identity tests and the send filters (`0x509b2` to `0x50b6c`)
  are `gps.md`, "Six or eight channels"; the reasons a track was lost
  or resumed, `Track lost [CK]` and the rest (`0x4da70` to
  `0x4dc5c`), and `GPS: Channel 7 Correlation` and `Channel 8` in
  `selftest_gps` (`0x17d94` → `0x17dee`) go with it.
- **About 86 SCPI handlers** for the paths below, most a few
  instructions, from `0x28d70` to `0x306f2`.
- **EEPROM.**  A 64-byte block at `0x4000c0`, checked by `0x32b62`
  (`nv_checksum`, `strncmp`); every later block 0x40 higher: the reset
  record `0x4001c0` → `0x400200`, the log pages `0x400d40` →
  `0x400d80`.  The settings slots grow from 128 to 160 bytes
  (`nv_checksum`, `nv_save`, `nv_load_slot`), the log from 180 entries
  to 177 (`log_init`, `log_append`).  The write-back of the settings
  record (`0x30c6c`) is `revisions.md`'s.
- Smaller: two calendar routines `leap_event_create` (`0x463c2` →
  `0x465ba`) calls (`0x11690`, `0x1170a`); the flag the loop sets
  while the reference is questionable (`0x44f48`, `revisions.md`,
  "Reference state"); a stage routine that sets the clock from the
  reference and rearms the interval (`0x46388`); `0x4396e` and
  `0x45c46` (`revisions.md`, "Holdover cause").

## What changed in what both have

By module, from the changed rows of the TSV: each count is the
primary's changed functions in the `modules.tsv` paths named.  Most
changes are the same code over moved fields; what is left:

- **`pll`** (30).  The loop block gains a field that moves its later
  offsets by two (`loop.md`, "In every image"); `pll_normal`,
  `startup_pll`, `holdover`, `pllp_task` and the stage routines differ
  mostly by that.  `pllp_task` no longer updates the lamps and the
  summary; `holdover` and `pll_init` no longer call
  `pwma_force_level`, which `system_init` now calls; the measurement
  reads set a flag on a 1 PPS fault (`revisions.md`, "Measurement
  errors").  The τ block and G are in `loop.md`.
- **`gps`** (48).  The GPS record grows (offsets 12 to 26 bytes
  higher), the request ring's header moves (+130 → +156), the message
  codes shift (`gps_request_class`, `gps_request_queue`: 48 → 55, 69 →
  80), and the decoders, the response matcher and the command builder
  go through `gps_message_supported` and the send filters.
  `gps_receiver_status_update` (`0x4b168` → `0x4b8a2`, 0.54) is the
  reference-state parser of `revisions.md`; `gpsm_task` (`0x4bf4a` →
  `0x4c9c0`) gains a request path (`0x4c57e`).
- **`scpi`** (42).  The `:SYSTem:COMMunicate:SER1` handlers test the
  port number (`cmpi.w #2`) and fail for other ports where the Z3805A's
  test for port 0; `console_port_read`, `scpi_session_init` and
  `scpi_format_response` go through the new port layer; the
  DIAGnostic identification fields moved.
- **`services/status`, `log`, `monitors`, `nv`, `records`** (43).
  Event and condition numbers shift (`efc_clamp` 66 → 72,
  `questionable_user_done` 77 → 91); the EEPROM moves above.
- **`screen`** (12).  The status screen lays out the 58503A's lines:
  the mode lines (`status_screen_mode_lines`), the time line, the
  satellite list.
- **Devices.**  `duart_init` (`0x1d93e` → `0x1d790`) no longer selects
  PORTGP's bits; the loopback tests pause between polls;
  `interpolator_calibrate`, `_sample` and `_verify`
  set an FPGA bit first (`fpga_set_bit`); `adc_read` (`0x1f2c0` →
  `0x1f0ec`) grows from 21 instructions to 32; `fpga_fields_to_registers`
  takes field indices up to 7 where the Z3805A's stops at 6;
  `eeprom_write` posts 79 for 73.
- **Boot and start-up.**  `boot_reset` and `primary_reset` program the
  SIM differently (below); `boot_duart_early_init` writes the DUART's
  ACR as `0xa0` for `0xb0`; `gpt_init` sets bits 0 and 1 of CFORC/PWMC
  (`0xfff924`) for bit 1; `boot_unpack_installer` has the 58503A's
  record offsets.
- **Calendar.**  `days_to_date` returns 31 December for the last day
  of a year (`revisions.md`, "Fixed in 3704"; the Z3805A has 3633's
  form), and `klok_run_due` runs a late periodic timer's action.

## Hardware

What the code shows; what each pin or device is was not traced unless
cited.

- **Chip selects.**  Both program the SIM alike (`restart.md`, "The
  chip selects"; not `README.md`'s table, which is the Z3816A's) but
  for CS4: the Z3805A's reset code sets CSBAR4 `0x5000`, 2 KB at
  `0x500000`, and CSOR4 `0x7830` (boot `0x5c8`, `0x5d0`; primary
  `0x14532`, `0x1453a`; MC68331UM D.3.25, D.3.26, `$YFFA5C` and
  `$YFFA5E`), where 3704 leaves CS4's base and option registers as
  reset leaves them, its select never asserted ("Chip select 4"
  below).  No instruction of either image addresses `0x500000`.
  Without those two writes 3704's later writes sit 0x10 lower: its
  boot code writes CSOR7 and CSBAR8 at `0x5f0` and `0x5f8`, where the
  Z3805A's are at `0x600` and `0x608`, the same values in both.
- **Clock.**  SYNCR is `0xcf00` in the Z3805A and `0xcf80` in 3704,
  boot code and primary: the same 16.777 MHz, with EDIV set, ECLK the
  system clock over 16 rather than 8 (MC68331UM, SYNCR, "EDIV").
- **Serial.**  The Z3805A uses the SCI and both DUART channels; 3704
  the DUART only, channel A for the GPS engine and channel B for the
  host.  The boot code's ACR goes from `0xb0` to `0xa0`: counter mode
  from X1/CLK over 16 becomes counter mode from channel B's 1X
  transmitter clock (MC68681 Table 4-4); bit 7, the baud-rate set, is
  set in both.  3704 programs the output port configuration (OPCR).
- **GPT.**  The Z3805A's time code and ES message run on TCNT, TIC1,
  TOC3 and TOC4 and the TIC2 interrupt; 3704 has no ES message, and so
  neither TOC4 nor the TIC2 interrupt, and sends the time code as the
  Z3805A does: `timecode_update` (`0x13da8`, from the 1PPS interrupt,
  `0x12f6e`) runs `timecode_schedule` (`0x140d0`), which sets TOC3 from
  TIC1 and TCNT and enables OC3's interrupt, through the routine
  `tcode_request` leaves in `tcode_send` (`0x103a86`).  `outputs.md`,
  "The FPGA bitstreams", has the OC4 timer, which drives no pin.
- **Ports.**  3704 sets or clears bit 5 of PORTF0 (`0xfffa19`) with
  the alarm and clears bit 3 (`0x40828`, "The alarm outputs" below);
  the Z3805A's code names PORTF0 only in the FPGA loader, which drives
  and reads DONE on bit 3 in both (`outputs.md`).  3704 also stops
  raising the outer oven's PGP5 (`ovens.md`).
- **The latch at `0x300000`.**  The Z3805A's five lamps (`outputs.md`,
  "The Z3805A's lamps") against 3704's three, GPS Lock, Holdover and
  Alarm on bits 0 to 2, and the front panel's two control lines on
  bits 6 and 7 (below).
- **The QSPI.**  Option 001's display and keypad on it in 3704, as
  QSPI device 0, beside the DACs and the ADC both have (below).
- **The FPGA.**  3704 loads the 58503A and Z3801A design, the Z3805A
  its own (`outputs.md`, "The FPGA bitstreams": 1868 of 3860 bytes
  differ; two pins configured otherwise, P14 and P45).  The loader and
  the readback check are the same code; the field table has an eighth
  field index.
- **The GPS engine.**  An Oncore in both, on the same `@@` messages,
  the 58503A able to take an eight-channel engine (`gps.md`).
- **The EEPROM.**  The same addresses, chip select 9 at `0x400000`
  in both, with the layout moved by the new block at `0x4000c0`.

The four sections below take the three unknowns this comparison left
(the front panel, PORTF0 bit 5 and the steady reads, chip select 4)
as far as 3704's code goes; "Not established" keeps what only the
bench can settle.  Pins are the MC68331's 132-pin QFP (MC68331UM Figure
3-2): MISO 43, MOSI 44, SCK 45, PCS0 to PCS3 46 to 49, PF5/IRQ5 73,
PC1/FC1/CS4 119.

### The front panel

The bench 58503A has Option 001 (`../bench.md`), the front-panel
display and keypad: a twelve-character vacuum-fluorescent display of
15-segment characters, the four lamps Power, GPS Lock, Holdover and
Alarm, and eight keys, each with its own lamp: Time, Long, Lat, Alt,
Sat, Status, Serial Port and Shift, Shift then Alt clearing the alarm
(097-58503-08, 1-2, 2-3 to 2-5).  The standard panel has the four
lamps alone (097-59551-02, 1-3).  Option 001 is retrofitted by
swapping the panel, which a ribbon cable with locking ears joins to
the main board, and loading firmware that supports it (097-58503-08,
2-3, 3-5).  3704 drives three of the lamps through the latch and the
display and keys over the QSPI.

*The four lamps.*  Bits 0, 1 and 2 of the latch at `0x300000` are GPS
Lock, Holdover and Alarm: `0x23b10` builds them from the flags
`:LED:GPSLock?`, `:LED:HOLDover?` and `:LED:ALARm?` read (`0x102840`,
`0x102841`, `0x102184`; handlers `0x2d994`, `0x2d9ae`, `0x2d8f2`), and
`0x23b50` writes them through the field setter `0x1373c`, from
`hmon_task` (`0x23b88`) every 51st pass and when `0x23b46` asks
(`0x23d40` to `0x23d70`).  `0x23b50` writes nothing while `lamp_test`
runs (`0x100e38`) or `:DIAGnostic:TMODe:STATe` is on (`0x101e76`).
Power is not the firmware's.  The Z3805A has GPS Lock and Holdover on
bits 4 and 5 instead (`outputs.md`, "The Z3805A's lamps").

*The device.*  The display and keys are QSPI device 0, which
`qspi_queue_setup` (`0x1f396`, case 0 at `0x1f3fc`) sets up as one
transfer: SPCR0 `0xa302`, master, 8 bits, CPOL 1 and CPHA 1 (SPI mode
3, the clock idling high), SPBR 2, so SCK is 16.777 MHz over 4,
4.19 MHz; command RAM `0x40`, BITSE with PCS3 to PCS0 all low; SPCR2
`0x8000`, one entry (MC68331UM D.4.10 to D.4.16).  So during the
panel's bytes the four PCS lines are low together, the one use of code
0 (`structures.md`, "The QSPI's devices").  Around each command the
code drives the latch's bits 6 and 7:

- `0x53736`, the start of every command: clears bit 6 and sets bit 7
  (`0x13702` at `0x53750`), pauses 2 ticks, exchanges the command byte
  through `qspi_read` (device 0) and pauses 2 ticks more.  It returns
  the byte that came back during the command byte.
- The data bytes follow through `qspi_write`, 2 ticks apart, their
  replies dropped.
- `0x53796`, the end: sets bit 6 (`0x136c0`) and spins 200 counts.
- `0x537b2`: clears bit 7, waits 1 s, sets it, waits 1 s.  Its one
  caller runs it between two self-tests (`0x48102`).

At 1024 ticks a second (`sleep_seconds`) 2 ticks is about 2 ms.  By
that use bit 6 frames a command, low from its first byte to its last,
and bit 7 is a reset the panel is held in while it is low; neither is
traced.  While `:DIAGnostic:TMODe:STATe` is on, every latch write
writes `:DIAGnostic:TMODe:DATA` alone (`0x136de`), bits 6 and 7 with
it, so the panel is cut off.

| Command | Then | Routine | Used for |
| ------- | ---- | ------- | -------- |
| `0x00` | nothing | `0x5395c` | a key poll |
| `0x01` | a lamp byte, then text to its NUL | `0x4812e` | a mode's line with its key's lamp |
| `0x02` | a lamp byte | `0x538d0` | the Status key's lamp, `0x20`, or none, during warm-up (`0x531a6`, `0x53286`) |
| `0x04` | text to its NUL | `0x5390a` | text with the lamps as they are: the warm-up's messages, the display test's sweep |
| `0x08`, `0x10` | nothing | `0x537ea` | the self-test, below |

*The keys.*  The byte returned with a command is a key: 0 none, 1 to
8 the keys in the order Time, Long, Lat, Alt, Sat, Status, Serial
Port, Shift, and key n's lamp is bit n − 1 of the lamp byte.
`0x53372`'s table (`0x533c6`) sets it: mode 1 the time with its
`GPS`, `UTC` or `LOCAL` label, lamp `0x01`; mode 2 a position field
with `E` or `W`, the longitude, `0x02`; mode 3 with `N` or `S`, the
latitude, `0x04` (`0x5274e` picks the letters by its flag); mode 4
`%+3.2fm`, the altitude, `0x08`; mode 5 `%d TRACKED`, `0x10`; mode 6
the status line (`0x5290c`), `0x20`; mode 7 `%d,%c,%d,%d`, the serial
settings, `0x40`; mode 8 `SHIFT`, `0x80`; mode 9 the alarm's text
(`0x29740`), `0x20`.  The key handler (`0x5362e`) makes a key from 1 to
8 the mode; 8, Shift, arms for 60 passes (`0x100e4a`), and 4, Alt,
while armed clears the alarm (`0x2f1ee`, which calls
`status_clear_events`, `*CLS`'s, then `ALRM CLEARED`; or `NO ALARM`
with none); while the alarm flag is set the mode is 9.  So the order
is the manual's and Shift then Alt is its alarm clear
(097-58503-08, 1-2, 2-5, and 2-9's note that the alarm's message has
priority).  Whether the byte answers the command it came with or the
one before is not settled by the code.

*When.*  `root_task` (`0x1321e`) runs the self-test quietly
(`0x48102(0)` at `0x13350`) and creates and starts the task `fpnl`
(`0x1342d`, entry `0x48018`, priority `0x5f`) only if it passes
(`0x1335a`); so a 58503A without Option 001 runs without the task.
`fpnl` blanks the display, waits 2 s and for `lamp_test` to finish,
then runs `0x52dea`: `HP` scrolls in from the right, 100 ticks a frame,
the key lamps sweeping out and back (lamp bytes `0x00`, `0x88`,
`0xcc`, `0xee`, `0xff` and back), the keys of the first twelve frames
OR'd together: 8 enters `DEMO MODE` (`0x52c16`), 5 `VFD DSP TEST`
(`0x52b1c`, every key lamp lit and `@;` stepped across the display)
and then the demonstration, as the manual's Shift and Sat-then-Time
do (097-58503-08, 2-5); by the OR, Sat alone gives 5 too.  It then
shows the warm-up's messages (`OCXO WARM UP`, `%01d SAT AQUIRD`,
`COARSE F ADJ`, ...) until `pll_mode` is 5 and `OUTPUT VALID` for 3 s.
After that `fpnl` waits up to 50 ticks at a time for the signal
`pps_interrupt` sends through `0x52732` (`0x12f74`): on the signal, or
after a key, it rewrites the mode's line; else it polls a key with
command 0.  So the panel's bus carries a line about once a second and
a poll about every 50 ms.  On the way to the installer the SCPI task
deletes `fpnl` and blanks the display (`0x130da`, from `0x294b4`).

*The self-test* (`0x537ea`): commands 0, 8, `0x10` and 0, each
closed, `busy_wait(5)` between; it passes if the byte back with
`0x10` is `0x55` and the one with the last 0 is `0xaa`, and with its
argument set prints both through `debug_printf`.  On a failure
`0x48102` runs `0x537b2` and tests once more.  Besides `root_task`,
only `fpnl` runs it, verbosely, when `0x100e4e` is set, which no
instruction does by its address (`0x480a8`, `0x480bc` read and clear
it).  A part that
answers `0x55` then `0xaa` is a controller on the panel's side, not a
bare shift register; which part, and what drives the 15-segment
display, the code cannot say.

### The alarm outputs

`0x29032`, which `image_diff` pairs with `status_update_rqs` by its
callers, calls `0x291f6` with the Status Byte (`status_byte`,
`0x102e60`), the Service Request Enable (`service_request_enable`,
`0x102e61`) and 2, its one call.  `0x291f6` computes the alarm as
Status Byte AND Enable, bit 6 left out, nonzero; sets bit 6, the
master summary, to it; and, its third argument being 2, stores it in
`status_request_service` (`0x1031d4`) and in the `:LED:ALARm?` flag
(`0x102184`), and calls `0x40828` with it.  That is 097-59551-02's
alarm: the Alarm Condition Register, `*STB?`, ANDed with the Alarm
Enable Register, `*SRE` (5-42, Figure 5-2; `*SRE` is 136 after a
preset, 5-43).

`0x40828`, with interrupts masked, reads PORTF0, clears bit 3, clears
bit 5 when the alarm is on and sets it when off, and writes it back;
then `fpga_set_bit(6, alarm)`, `fpga_bit_table`'s seventh entry
(`0x413c4`): register 3, mask `0x04`, so bit 2 of the FPGA's register
at `0x304003`.  The reset code makes PF5 an output, high (PFPAR
`0x04`, DDRF `0xf7`, PORTF0 `0xf7` at `0x658` to `0x668` and
`0x1462c` to `0x1463c`; MC68331UM D.3.9 to D.3.11, Table D-5), and
nothing else writes it: the FPGA loader moves PF3, PF6 and PF7 only
(`0x2260c`, `0x230c8`).  The Z3805A's
table has no entry 6.

So one alarm drives three things at once: PF5, low while on (U33 pin
73); FPGA register 3 bit 2, set while on; and the Alarm lamp, latch bit
2, through the flag.  097-59551-02 gives the 58503A a rear-panel Alarm
(TTL) output that "goes on and off with the Alarm indicator" (1-4) and
the 59551A an Alarm BITE relay that "opens and closes with" it (1-2),
which `*CLS` sets "inactive/off" with the lamp (5-40).  PF5 and the
FPGA bit are the two candidates for the rear output; the code cannot
say which, nor what the other drives.

### Reads until steady

Three routines read a port until its masked bits hold, and return 1
if any is set: PORTE (`0x23464`, ten equal reads in a row within
1000), PORTF0 (`0x234ae`, the same) and PORTGP (`0x234f8`, four within
100).  None has a caller or a pointer to it in 3704, nor their 3633
copies (`0x23220`, `0x2326a`, `0x232b4`) in 3633; `image_diff` missed
`0x23464`, which follows a string.  They are the pattern of the
Z3805A's unreferenced `duart_input_*_debounced` (`0x1e072` to
`0x1e0ea`), for the processor's own ports.  So no 58503A image
reads PORTF0 bit 5 as an input, or debounces anything: the only reads
of PORTF0 are the FPGA loader's of DONE, PF3, and `0x40828`'s
read-modify-write.  What the masks would have been is not in the
image.

### Chip select 4

CSPAR0's field CSPA0[5], bits 11 and 10, sets the CS4 pin: 00 a
discrete output, PC1, at PORTC's bit 1; 10 an 8-bit chip select
(MC68331UM D.3.23, Tables D-8 and D-10).  3633's boot code writes
CSPAR0 `0x23ff` (`0x568`), 00, and its primary `0x2bff` (`0x143c4`),
10; 3704 writes `0x2bff` in both (`0x568`, `0x1453c`), as the Z3805A
does.  No 58503A image writes PORTC (`0xfffa41`) or CSBAR4 and CSOR4,
so:

- under 3633's boot code PC1 drives PORTC's reset value, 1 (D.3.22);
- as a chip select, in 3704 from the boot code on and in 3633 from
  its primary on, CS4's BYTE field keeps its reset value, disabled,
  "so that a chip-select signal cannot be asserted" (4.8.4).

Between reset and the first CSPAR0 write the field's high bit comes
from DATA2 (4.8.4): a few instructions as FC1 or as the same disabled
select.  So on every 58503A image the pin sits high and carries
nothing the firmware makes; the Z3805A's CS4, 2 KiB at `0x500000`, is
set up and never addressed.  Whether the board connects pin 119 to
anything is the bench's.

## The port

## What the 58503A's C does otherwise

Where the 58503A's code differs from the Z3805A's, its C is written to
the 58503A's, by subject.

*The identification.*  `idn_model_default` and
`:DIAGnostic:IDENtification:DEFault?` give the model `58503A`
(`0x32ffa`); the identification record's and the NV register's tag is
`CQ` (`records.h`'s `RECORDS_TAG`, `0x33001`), which `DEFault?` also
gives as its suffix; the firmware revision `*IDN?` gives and the NV
register stores is `3704` (`revision_text`, `0x1309c`); and the
installer's `*IDN?` names `USA` (`installer_revision_text`,
`installer::0x100cde`), where the Z3805A's names `Peru`.

*The health monitor.*

- `monitors.c` defines `hmon_channels` (`0x2238a`) with the image's
  entries, so that the export declares it `const` and its routines are
  gcc's: seven convert routines (`hmon_convert_temperature` to
  `hmon_convert_antenna_current`, `0x21b48` to `0x21c88`), the
  supplies' and the oven current's report routines (`0x22194`,
  `0x221aa`), the oven current's check (`hmon_check_pll_stage`,
  `0x221c4`) and `monitor_average_add` (`0x21d34`), which in the 58503A
  takes the channel.  Its happenings are `status_condition_code`s.
- Written by hand: `hmon_channel_read` (`0x21ca2`), whose reading drops
  the convert routine's argument and result; `hmon_channel_step`
  (`0x2220a`), whose reading takes its channel from D2 where the image
  passes `hmon_channel_index` (`0x101e6f`); `hmon_check_pll_stage`, 1
  while `pll.stage` is 3 to 6, whose `moveq #1,d0` the decompiler read
  as a float's bits, `(uint8_t)(float)1.4013e-45`, always 0.  No other
  float literal of that kind is in either image's compiled C.
- `hmon_task` (`0x23b88`), written from the Z3805A's, as it reads D5
  as its caller left it: it steps the channels every tenth pass, takes
  the GPS 1PPS's failure from the GPS reference's record, withholds it
  while `gps_pps_unset_flag` (`0x102000`), which nothing sets, is set,
  and writes the lamps every 51st pass and when `lamps_requested` asks
  (`0x23b10`, `0x23b50`), its heartbeat through `board_leds_change`
  (`0x137a4`).  `gps_pps_monitor_reset` clears `gps_pps_unset_flag`
  where the Z3805A's clears the skipped checks.
- `watchdog_report` (`0x2369e`) is the Z3805A's hand-written C: the
  decompiler's leaves out the move back to its caller's status
  register, so that a task calling it would stay in supervisor mode.

*The status screen.*  `status_screen` (`0x4af48`),
`status_screen_position` (`0x4a562`) and `status_screen_tfom`
(`0x4a8a6`) are written from the Z3805A's.  The 58503A's screen has a
satellite row for each channel, seven at least, and a line less above
synchronization for eight; the position column's first row is the GPS
1PPS's state (`status_screen_sync`, `0x4a1fa`) and its eighth the
height, which `status_screen_position` reports through a fourth
argument; "Locked to GPS" or "to Ext", the 1PPS TI "relative to" GPS
or Ext and the holdover reasons name the reference in use; the health
line tests the oven supply's bit 4 alone, the EFC's bits 6 and 7 and
the GPS receiver's 8 and 9 (`bit_4_set`, `bits_6_7_any`,
`bits_8_9_any`); and a row the TFOM column has none for is empty.
`satellite_list_limits` (`0x2d154`) lists at most eight satellites
tracked, the Z3805A's six (`gps.h`'s `GPS_CHANNELS` is 8;
`gps.channel_count` is 6 or 8).

*The GPS.*

- The 58503A prints "(no commands were sent since last read)" where
  the Z3805A prints "were issued" (`gps_print_command_log`,
  `0x4d432`).
- `gps_diag_sequence` sends the link reset as `0x50` and the engine's
  self-test script as `0x55`, the Z3805A's `0x45` and `0x4a`;
  `gps_request_build` is under `gps_request_code` ("The enums").
- `oncore_receiver_id_value` (`0x509b2`) finds a receiver ID line by
  its index in `oncore_receiver_id_lines` (`0x5262c`, 12 bytes an
  entry: the line's text in `gps.receiver_id`, the label it begins
  with, the label's length) and returns the text after the label;
  `gps_receiver_abilities_update` (`0x50a16`) reads the options' (an
  `I`, the Time RAIM option) and the software version's and revision's
  (8.4) into `gps.field_592`.
- `oncore_filter_channels` (`0x50ad8`), the send filter, which calls
  its message's first encoder, and `oncore_filter_traim` (`0x50b48`)
  are written by hand (`gps_requests.c`).

*The port layer and SCPI.*

- The console port's object is at `0x29090`, its method table at
  `0x2904c` (`scpi_console_port`, `0x28e5c`; `scpi.h`'s
  `SCPI_CONSOLE_PORT`).
- `:DIAGnostic:ME`'s setter (`0x2b2de`) answers an undefined header, as
  its query does; the Z3805A's does nothing.
- `:SYSTem:COMMunicate:ALARm:RECeive:BAUD?` (`0x30188`) answers DUART
  channel B's rate for the path word 2 (ALARm, BOOT, DRTB, PFORth,
  SERial, TOD), an undefined header for any other; written by hand
  (`scpi_system.c`), its two answers being tails the compiler merged.

*The process reports.*  `os_report_regions` (`0x16882`) is the
Z3805A's, relocated, and its hand-written C stands: the decompiler's
reading took 18 bytes more of the console task's stack than the image.
The process report prints, as the Z3805A's, the `"%s"` address
`os_report_processes` keeps in D5 (`0x167c2`) as a task's stack share
("The function check", the known).

*The start.*  `root_task` (`0x1321e`, `main.c`) erases the log when
`nv_settings_defaulted` (`0x102276`) is set, which `nv_restore` sets
when neither slot loads (`0x327dc`), where the Z3805A's reads
`selftest.eeprom`; it creates the front panel's task, `fpnl`
(`task_fpnl`, `0x1039cc`), only where `front_panel_check` finds the

*Channel B.*  `drtb_gets`'s given cases with the echo off name the
58503A's `duartb_echo_none` (`0x1d3d0`), and the console sessions' echo
is that address too: the Z3805A's (`0x1d570`) is inside other code in
the 58503A, where the image's copy takes a bus error.

*The front panel.*

- `front_panel_task` (`0x48018`) writes QSPI minor device 0, in the

  Z3805A's sends three of 12 bits.
- `front_panel_format_dms` (`0x5274e`) is `format_dms_precise`
  (`0x48364`) with `"%c%4d:%02d:%06.3f"`, the seconds to the
  millisecond, its angle a double at `+8`; both are written around one

  (latitude 0) and the Lat key's (1), the angle `survey_position` or

- `front_panel_status_text` (`0x5290c`), the Status key's line,

## The function check

## Time Reset and the Oncore's time

Questionable bit 0, Time Reset, an event: "the Receiver reset its time
because the Receiver's time was found to be different from the time
being reported by the satellites" (097-59551-02, 5-40).  It latches
until `:STATus:QUEStionable:EVENt?` reads it (5-46), `*CLS` (5-40) or
power-up; nothing on the front panel clears it.

*The path.*  `clock_check_against_gps_58503a` (`0x43f20`) compares the
clock's date and time (`get_gps_datetime`) with the Oncore's, `@@Ea`'s
`gps.status.time` and `.date`.  The first mismatch sets
`clock_gps_mismatch` (`0x102875`); the second in a row calls
`clock_set` from the Oncore's time and `status_happening(STATUS_TIME_SET)`
(0x5a, `0x43f98`), and clears the flag.  Beyond the Z3805A's, it counts
no mismatch while the Oncore's time equals `pll.reference_time`
(`+0x3d8`, `pllp_task`'s copy, `0x47c60`), and it reports nothing when
the byte at `0x102876`, set by `pll_restart` (`0x47716`) entering
powerup recovery, is set and the times agree.

*When.*  `startup_pll` (`0x4429e`) and `pll_normal` (`0x4495a`) call it
each second while `clock_gps_mismatch` is set, else when the clock's
second is 14, with the reference good, not questionable, and no leap
acknowledgment pending; a check that has just set the clock asks for
`gps_leap_ack`.  `powerup` (`0x46072`) and `holdover_recovery`
(`0x43fc0`) call it once their delays end.  The clock keeps UTC with
`:DIAG:GPS:UTC` 1, as this unit's EEPROM has
(`eeprom/58503a-3710A01056`), and `@@Aw` then puts the Oncore in UTC:
locked, a mismatch is seen at hh:mm:14 UTC and the time reset at
hh:mm:15 at the earliest.

*Leap seconds.*  The 58503A decodes none of the almanac's leap fields
(WN_LSF, DN, delta t_LS, delta t_LSF).  `gps_leap_sequence`
(`0x46f64`) asks `@@Bj`, the Oncore's pending flag; `leap_event_create`
(`0x465ba`) acts only when it is nonzero, and books the leap for
`leap_opportunity_date`, a quarter's end; `leap_second_near`
(`0x117c6`) and `time_next_second` (`0x1138a`) gate on quarter-end
dates and `nv_settings.leap_pending`.  `gps_leap_ack` (`0x4703a`)
measures GPS-UTC by switching `@@Aw` and differencing.  Any comparison
of an 8-bit week is inside the Oncore VP, not in this image.

*2026-10-25.*  On the bench 58503A (3704) `*STB?` read 72 (Questionable
summary and master summary, 5-44) at about 00:00:15 UTC, just after
GPS week 2441 ended; it stayed locked, TI, EFC, TFOM and satellites
unchanged, and the diagnostic log gained nothing.

Observed, in the daemon's per-second `time_utc` against host time: the
receiver's time of day stepped back 1 s at 00:00:15 UTC (it repeated
00:00:15; Time Reset latched), then forward 1 s at about 00:08:15 UTC,
a second reset while the event was already latched, and has been right
since.  Both steps fall on the :15 reset above.  Time of day was 1 s
slow for about 8 minutes; 1 PPS phase and 10 MHz were unaffected.

Cause, strongly supported but **unconfirmed** (the Oncore VP's firmware
is not to hand): the Oncore's own time went 1 s behind at the end of
week 2441, as a phantom leap-second insertion would.  Week 2441 mod 256
is 137, the 8-bit WN_LSF the almanac has carried since the 2016-12-31
leap (week 1929), and the receiver's epoch-wrapped week is 1417; an
Oncore that matches WN_LSF and DN 7 against its own 8-bit week would
insert a leap there.  Its recovery about 8 minutes later plausibly
came with the next UTC parameters (subframe 4 page 18, every
12.5 minutes); not proven.  The 10-04, 10-11 and 10-18 boundaries
passed cleanly.  The firmware run in an emulator with this EEPROM and
a synthetic Oncore (`@@Bj` 0, no WN_LSF model), from 2026-10-24 23:35 UTC, stayed
at `*STB?` 0 through 00:25: a negative control only, the 58503A's
firmware alone does not step.  If right, it recurs at the end of week
2697, about 2031-09-21.

*The Z3805A 3543B* has the same mechanism.  `clock_check_against_gps`
(`0x43f18`) compares with the six-channel `@@Ba`'s time, sets the clock
on the second mismatch and raises `STATUS_TIME_SET` (0x4c), Questionable
bit 0 as on the Z3801A ("Time has been set", 097-z3801-01, 4-15);
`startup_pll` (`0x4429e`) and `pll_normal` (`0x448fc`) check at second 14
or after a mismatch, `powerup` and `holdover_recovery` (`0x43f9a`)
after their delays.  It lacks the 58503A's reference-time,
questionable-reference and restart guards.  Its leap second is likewise
`@@Bj` only: `gps_leap_sequence` (`0x46d98`), `leap_event_create`
(`0x463c2`) to the quarter's end, `leap_second_near` (`0x11680`),
`time_next_second` (`0x11300`), and `gps_leap_ack` (`0x46e70`) for the
offset.  So a Z3805A on an Oncore with the same fault would latch the
same way.
