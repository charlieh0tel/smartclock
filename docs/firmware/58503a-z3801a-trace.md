# The 58503A/Z3801A FPGA

Traced against the Z3805A's design (`z3805a-trace.md`), the reference,
as the differences from it.  The 58503A and the Z3801A load one
bitstream, byte for byte (`outputs.md`, "The FPGA bitstreams").

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Images that load it | 58503A 3633 and 3704, Z3801A 3543 |
| Bitstream | `third_party/firmware/images/fpga/58503a-z3801a.bit` |
| Part | XC3042, PLCC84 |
| Logic blocks, I/O blocks, nets | 87 of 144, 35, 166 (`tools/fpga/ref/58503a-z3801a.stats`) |
| Clocks | one global clock buffer, GCLK, from P7 as in the Z3805A, and local clocks on block outputs and P82 |
| Oscillator the divide implies | 10 MHz (a divide by 10,000,000) |
| 1 PPS output pins | P83, P78 |
| Other outputs | the Z3805A's, but P14 an unregistered passthrough of P17 and no P45 ("Other outputs") |
| Differences from the reference | the 1 PPS output's width logic (EI, HJ); P45's 1 PPS dropped and P14's replaced; the rest AL's and DH's flip-flops exchanged and one flip-flop moved (HK) |

Up to the exchange of AL's and DH's flip-flops and the move of one
(HK), the design is the Z3805A's but for the 1 PPS
output's width logic (EI and HJ), P14 and P45 (**read**, below).  Its
count datapath reads out as the Z3805A's: **simulated** (`make -f
tools/fpga/Makefile check-readings-58503a-z3801a.fast`), with the
preset on it reads the count `0x555555` and the status `0x04`, and the
whole read map matches the Z3805A's and the 58503B's (the count's
nibbles `0x05` at `0x12` to `0x17`), with the interpolator's byte at
`0x5a` and at `0x1c` (`check-readings-58503a-z3801a.byte_1c`), so the
count, read port and interpolator interface read out alike, and `58503b-trace.md` traces
that same datapath.

## Method and confidence

How the designs are decoded and simulated, and how far either can be
trusted, is `fpga-comparison.md`'s ("Provenance"), as are the glossary
and the confidence words.

    make -f tools/fpga/Makefile 58503a-z3801a                   # decode, build, the fast run (+seconds=0)
    make -f tools/fpga/Makefile check-readings-58503a-z3801a.fast    # +seconds=0 +registers +probe: the read map
    make -f tools/fpga/Makefile check-readings-58503a-z3801a.byte_1c # +seconds=0 +registers +byte=1c +probe: the read map with the interpolator at 0x1c
    make -f tools/fpga/Makefile check-readings-58503a-z3801a.three   # +seconds=3 +interpolator +probe: the 1 PPS edges, the readings
    make -f tools/fpga/Makefile check-readings-58503a-z3801a.sync1   # +seconds=2 +sync=1 +probe: the sync field (also .sync2, .sync3)

Every simulated figure quoted here, unless it names another run, is
printed by one of those runs,
which diff their output against
`tools/fpga/ref/58503a-z3801a.<run>.readings`; the runs and their flags
are the Makefile's `READ_` variables.

What differs from the Z3805A is **read** from the two netlists compared
block by block (`tools/fpga/tilediff.py target/fpga/z3805a.json
target/fpga/58503a-z3801a.json`, which `make -f tools/fpga/Makefile
check` diffs against `tools/fpga/ref/58503a-z3801a.tilediff`): ten
logic blocks are configured otherwise (AB, AC, AE, AL, BB, CD, DH, ED,
EI, HJ), one is added (HK), and eleven more are configured alike but
read another net (AD, BC, BD, BE, CB, CC, DB, DC, DD, EE, FJ: AL.Y for
AL.X, and FJ HK.X for HJ.Y).  Of the I/O blocks, P14's (PAD95) is
configured otherwise, P45's (PAD59) is removed, and four read another
net (PAD29, PAD30, PAD34, PAD36: P71, P70, P67, P65).  A tile name is
a position, not a function; the comparison by name means something
here because the two designs place the logic they share in the same
blocks.

Most of those differences are one: AL's two outputs exchanged
(below), with every load of either moved to the other.  That accounts
for AB, AC, AE, BB, CD and ED, the eleven blocks that read another
net but FJ, and the four I/O blocks.  DH's two flip-flops are
exchanged with no change of function, and HK holds the Z3805A's HJ.Y
flip-flop, which FJ reads.  The one change of behavior inside the
logic is EI and the new HJ, the 1 PPS output's width.

## Clocks

P7 drives the global clock buffer, GCLK, as in the Z3805A
(`z3805a-trace.md`, "Clocks"), which clocks 45 logic blocks
(`tools/fpga/ref/58503a-z3801a.stats`, `clock_loads`), the Z3805A's
and HK, and the output flip-flops of P83 and P78 (**read**).  Other
flip-flops are clocked by nets of their own, as in the Z3805A: P82
(PAD15.I) clocks AH, the start's capture; the tap, GH.X, clocks CI, the
stop; CH.Y clocks BH; each of the count's stages after bit 0 is clocked
by the stage before it, a ripple; and AL.X and AL.Y clock the data
bus's output flip-flops (**read**).

## Register port

The Z3805A's, with the read map simulated above.  (**read**)

- **AB, AC, AE**, the interpolator-input read and decode cluster that
  feeds the count and status read multiplexer, reading the interpolator
  pads (PAD1 and PAD2, PAD5 and PAD6, PAD10 and PAD11), read AL.Y where
  the Z3805A's read AL.X.  Each look-up table is the Z3805A's function
  with its inputs permuted: with this design's AL.Y taken for the
  Z3805A's AL.X, the truth tables match.
- **P71, P70, P67, P65**, the data bus's DATA bits 0 to 3 (the
  testbench's pin map, `tools/fpga/58503a-z3801a_tb.v`), clock their
  output flip-flops on AL.Y, AL.X, AL.Y, AL.X where the Z3805A's clock
  them on AL.X, AL.Y, AL.X, AL.Y: the same nets under AL's exchanged
  names.

The write decode is the Z3805A's: registers 0, 1 and 3, all with
ADDR2 Low (EL.G register 0, DL register 1, EL.F register 3), and none
for register 2 or 4 to 7, so a write there reaches no logic.  Register
3's one latch, DI, takes DATA bits 0 and 1 (P71, P70), the sync field
("The sync field").  P67, DATA bit 2, has no input path at all: its I/O
block is an output only.  So the 58503A 3704's alarm, which it writes
to register 3's bit 2 (`outputs.md`, "What the 58503A drives";
`58503a-3704.md`, "The alarm outputs"), and the Z3801A 3543's four
flags in register 2 (`z3801a-3543.md`), reach no FPGA logic.  That is
the bitstream's; whether the board takes either from the bus by some
other path is not read here.

## The count

(**read**)

- **AL**, the GCLK load and read-strobe toggle, is the Z3805A's
  two-flip-flop toggle with its X and Y exchanged: `QX <- ~QY`,
  `QY <- QX`, `AL.X = ~QY`, `AL.Y = QY` here against the Z3805A's
  `QX <- QY`, `QY <- ~QX`, `AL.X = QX`, `AL.Y = ~QX`.  So this
  design's AL.Y is the Z3805A's AL.X, and its AL.X the Z3805A's AL.Y.
- **BB, CD, ED**, count and readout stages that take AL's output,
  read AL.Y where the Z3805A reads AL.X.  BB reads it on its B input
  where the Z3805A's reads it on C, its D input (EB.Y) unchanged; CD
  and ED read it on E where the Z3805A's read it on D.  Each look-up
  table is the Z3805A's function with its inputs permuted, so the count
  logic is the same.

## Start, stop and status bits

The Z3805A's: AH, clocked by P82, the start's capture, and CI, clocked
by the tap, the stop, are unchanged.  (**read**)

## The 1 PPS divider

(**read**)

The divider and its terminal count are the Z3805A's: HH is unchanged,
and HH.X, every 10,000,000 clocks, is the terminal, which sets EI,
clears the sync pending (EK) and enables the seconds counter (IK, IL).
What differs is how long the 1 PPS output stays High:

- **EI**, the internal 1 PPS's output driver, whose EI.Y drives P83's
  and P78's output registers, holds the pulse, through P83's own input
  (PAD14.I), while either of two nets is Low:
  `EI.F = HH_X + ~HJ_X·PAD14_I + PAD14_I·~KH_X` against the Z3805A's
  `HH_X + PAD14_I·~KH_Y`, a new term and KH.X for KH.Y.
- **HJ** is new logic: a registered four-input AND of divider nets
  (`IG_Y·GI_Y·JH_Y·IH_X`, its flip-flop on GCLK), whose one load is
  EI.B, that hold term.  The Z3805A's HJ registers JI.X (`HJ.F =
  JI_X` into its Y flip-flop) for FJ.
- **HK**, the added block, holds that flip-flop here: it registers
  JI.X (`HK.G = JI_X` into its X flip-flop), and FJ reads HK.X.

Both changes, the HJ term and KH.X for KH.Y, are needed for this
design's width (**inferred** from the equations; no run makes one
without the other): the Z3805A's P83 stays High 314 clocks, this
one's 260 (the simulated widths below).

**The divide and the tap** (**simulated**,
`check-readings-58503a-z3801a.three`): P83 and P78 rise together
exactly 1,000,000,000 ns apart, the first at 1.000000952 s, a divide
by 10,000,000 as the Z3805A's, and stay High 26,000 ns, where the
Z3805A's stay High 31,400 ns (`check-readings-z3805a.three`).  The
tap, HG.Y, rises 400,000,000 ns, 4,000,000 clocks, after P83, and the
count with the GPS 1 PPS at 0.250000037 s reads 1,500,012 over the
preset each second, the Z3805A's reading: the tap and the stop's
pipeline are the firmware's 0.4000003 s calibration, as in the Z3805A
(`counter.md`, "Which 1 PPS edges").  HH.X, the terminal, has pulses
of a block delay or two that are the model's hazards, not the design's
(as the Z3805A's: `outputs.md`, "Simulation"); the probe removes them
and its HH.X line says how many: `58503a-z3801a.three`, 2 pulses under
5 ns removed, the first at 0.900000953 s, one a second.

**Two fewer 1 PPS output pins.**  The Z3805A registers the internal
1 PPS on P83, P78, P45 and P14 (`z3805a-trace.md`); the 58503A
registers it on **P83 and P78 only** (both registered, from EI.Y).
Its P14 (PAD95) is an output, unregistered, from P17 (PAD91), not a
1 PPS, and P45's I/O block is unused, so no 1 PPS.  (The 58503B
makes P14 a registered pulse on alternate seconds and adds P19, a
GCLK / 65 output: `58503b-trace.md`.)

## The sync field

The Z3805A's (`z3805a-trace.md`, "The sync field"), **read**: DI, DJ,
EK, EJ, GI, GJ, GK and HH are unchanged, and EJ, the restart, reads
the same nets (DH.X, EK.X, DI.X, DI.Y).  DH, the GPS 1 PPS's (P82,
PAD15) rising-edge detector, whose one load is EJ's B input, is the
Z3805A's with its two flip-flops exchanged: here `QY <- PAD15_I`,
`QX <- QY`, `DH.X = ~QX·QY`, against `QX <- PAD15_I`, `QY <- QX`,
`DH.X = QX·~QY`, the same one-clock pulse on the GPS 1 PPS's rising
edge.

**Simulated** (`check-readings-58503a-z3801a.sync1`, `.sync2` and
`.sync3`: `+seconds=2 +sync=KIND`, the field written 0 then the kind
on the first arm, as `counter_arm` does), the Z3805A's results:

- **Kind 1** moves the next internal 1 PPS one clock, 100 ns, earlier:
  P83 and P78 rise at 1.000000852 s, where `58503a-z3801a.three`'s rise
  at 1.000000952 s, and the count after it reads 1,500,011 over the
  preset for 1,500,012.  GJ.Y, kind 1's step line, is High once,
  15,498.0 ns from 0.999985355 s, in the second's last millisecond (and
  has one pulse under 5 ns removed, at 0.999750954 s, a model hazard); EK.X,
  the pending sync, is High 999,940,000.0 ns from 0.000060852 s, until
  that terminal count clears it.
- **Kind 2** moves it one clock later: P83 and P78 at 1.000001052 s, the
  count 1,500,013; GJ.X, kind 2's step line, High once, 15,698.0 ns
  from 0.999985355 s (one pulse under 5 ns removed, at 0.999750954 s); EK.X High 999,940,200.0 ns.
- **Kind 3** restarts the divider on the next GPS 1 PPS: EJ.X, the
  restart, is High 100.0 ns, 115.0 ns after the GPS 1 PPS at
  0.250000037 s, so on the second GCLK edge after it, and EK.X clears
  with it (High 249,939,300.0 ns).  No internal 1 PPS comes at 1.0 s;
  P83 and P78 rise at 1.250000152 s, 10,000,000 clocks after the
  restart, 115.0 ns after that second's GPS 1 PPS, and both counts read
  4,000,004 over the preset.

## Confidence

**Simulated:** the preset read `0x555555`, the status `0x04` and the
read map, the Z3805A's and the 58503B's; the divide by 10,000,000, the
1 PPS width, the tap 4,000,000 clocks after the 1 PPS, and the
readings; sync kinds 1 and 2 stepping the internal 1 PPS a clock
earlier and later, and kind 3 restarting it on the GPS 1 PPS, as the
Z3805A's.

**Read:** the ten differing logic blocks and the added one, the four
I/O blocks on AL's exchanged outputs and P14's and P45's, and that
all but EI and HJ are the Z3805A's logic; the write decode, register
3's two bits, and P67's lack of an input path; the sync field's
blocks, the Z3805A's.

**Open:**

- what P14's passthrough of P17 is for;
- whether the 58503A's register 3 bit 2 alarm or the Z3801A's
  register 2 flags reach anything on the board by a path outside the
  FPGA;
- everything on a board, and the pins' roles, read from the logic.
