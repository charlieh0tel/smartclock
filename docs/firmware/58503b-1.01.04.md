# 58503B 1.01.04 against Z3817A 3752 and Z3816A 4001

The 58503B's image, `third_party/firmware/images/58503b-1.01.04.bin`
(revision 1.01.04, `*IDN?` answering `1.01.04-D`), rebuilt from the
console log of another owner's unit, serial L85376, which printed all
512 KB of its flash (`58503b-L85376-console.log`; `third_party/NOTICE`).
The 58503B is the 58503A's successor, a GPS time and frequency
reference with a Motorola Oncore engine.  Its folder is
`third_party/firmware/analysis/58503b-1.01.04/`.  The first wave seeded
its names from the Z3817A 3752's, and for the Oncore code the Z3817A
has no counterpart of from the 58503A 3704's as a second base, as the
Z3817A's were seeded from the Z3815A's and the 58503A's
([`z3817a-3752.md`](z3817a-3752.md)).  The second, once the Z3816A 4001
was seeded ([`z3816a-4001.md`](z3816a-4001.md)), measured it as a base,
kept the Z3817A and took the Z3816A as the second base.  The waves
after brought the function check to no failures and wrote by hand what
no other image's C gives it, and the join made it the seventh image to
share `common/`, every function of it written by hand ("Joining
`common/`").

What is read from the image is marked **read**, what follows from it
**inferred**; "Not established" lists what neither settles.  Addresses
are the 58503B's unless another image is named.

The Z3817A and the Z3816A carry the primary all but equally: the
Z3817A's matches carry one function more (identical and relocated),
the Z3816A's a share a tenth of a point higher, of four fewer functions
matched or new, and more of the bytes.  The base is the Z3817A, whose
matches carry the most functions, the rule `PLAN.md` records
("Seeding a port's names"); its first 128 KiB, the boot code and the

the Z3815A's differ from both in the four bytes at `0x4000`), where the

seeding left more of the 58503B's functions unnamed and paired several
otherwise (`bit_4_set` and `bit_12_set`, which the Z3817A and the

58503B's RAM (`pll`, `efc_calibration`, `log_cursor` among them): the

The Z3816A is the port's second base (`seed_names.py`'s

matches where those carry it and its name is free, with its local

only the second base's own pairing being a lead does
(`Seeding.second_rows`).  Most of what the Z3816A carries it carries
unnamed, its first wave having named little past the 58503A's names.
The Z3817A's own Oncore code is the 58503A's, named from it the same
way, so most of the 58503B's Oncore code comes through the Z3817A's
rows.

What the first wave carried from the 58503A as its second base and
neither the Z3817A nor the Z3816A carries named, the front panel's
routines, `pwma_force_level`, `alarm_outputs_set`, `strncat`,
`hmon_convert_oven_unused` and six tables, are hand rows now, each
note giving the 58503A's match.  `0x2e994`, which the 58503A's matches
named `duart_input_port_read`, is not one of them: the 58503A's reads
the DUART's input port, where the 58503B's returns the byte
`sci_release_for_timecode` sets (`0x103efb`, **read**).

Its types started as the Z3817A's `smartclock.h` and `check-layout.c`
(`rewritten/58503b-1.01.04/types/`); the records its export needed
are the 58503B's own (below, "Types"), the rest's layouts, addresses
and comments still the Z3817A's.

Three cautions for anyone reading the seeded names or a Ghidra listing
made with them:

- **No DUART.**  The 58503B's part at `0x200000` is an ACIA, not a
  68681 (`outputs.md`, "The heartbeat"; below, "Layout and chip
  selects").  The second wave named its routines and the SCI's for
  the part they drive ("The SCI and the ACIA"); what keeps a DUART
  name there says why.
- Records still typed with the Z3817A's `smartclock.h` are its layouts
  over the 58503B's bytes: a field name in a listing says where the
  Z3817A keeps that field, not what the 58503B's byte there is.
- A changed or lead row keeps the Z3817A's name for code that may do
  something else here; each row's note gives the similarity.

## Layout and chip selects

**Read.**  The Z3817A's layout, the same bytes: the boot code and the
Vatican installer in the first 128 KiB, the installer's records at `0x8000`, unpacked to `0x100400` and entered at
`0x1013a6`, the primary's vectors at `0x20000`.  Its checksum is the
Z3816A's sum of words (`restart.md`, "The installer", "Boot check").

The chip selects ([`chip-selects.md`](chip-selects.md), "58503B
1.01.04"): the boot code's two runs are the Z3817A's.  The primary's
map differs from the Z3817A's primary in three selects:

- **CS8, `0x200000`**: CSOR8 `0xb830`, an 8-bit select synchronous on
  ECLK, a 6800-bus part's timing, where the Z3817A's `0x3bf0` waits for
  the DUART's DSACK.  Its driver's messages name an ACIA
  (`0x2d5ca` to `0x2d64c`).
- **CS4, the level 2 interrupt acknowledge**: CSOR4 `0x7805`,
  autovectored, where the Z3817A's `0x2bc4` takes the DUART's vector.
- **CS7, `0x302000`**: an 8-bit read-only window, where the Z3817A's
  is a write-only one at `0x306000`.  The one read of it is at
  `0x2dbc8`, the end of `efc_write` (`0x2dba0`), after a QSPI transfer and a pulse on PE7
  (bit 7 of PORTE0, `0xfffa11`, cleared then set); a hand row labels
  the byte `cs7_window`.

The heartbeat is bit 3 of the latch at `0x300000`, as no other image's
(`outputs.md`, "The heartbeat").

## The SCI and the ACIA

**Read.**  The 58503B has no DUART: its host port is the SCI, and the
Oncore engine's link is the ACIA at `0x200000` ("Layout and chip
selects").  The routines the matches carry under DUART names are
named for the part they drive, by hand rows, each note giving the
name its matches carry:

- The ACIA's, between `0x2d2f4` and `0x2d582`: `acia_send_message` and
  `acia_get_byte`, the firmware's own names (its messages "wait_v
  SIG_SOQA error in acia_send_message" and "wait_v SIG_SIQ error in
  acia_get_byte", `0x2d59f`, `0x2d626`), `acia_isr` ("ACIA isr
  signaling SIG_SIQ", `0x2d64c`), `acia_rx_pending` and
  `acia_tx_interrupt`; its driver state `acia` (`0x103d9c`) and receive
  ring `acia_ring` (`0x103c99`), which its matches name `duarta` and
  `duarta_ring`.
- The SCI's, whose messages name it (`0x2e9e7` to `0x2eb9e`):
  `sci_init`, the device's init, which creates `sciR` and `sciW`;
  `sci_gets` and `sci_read`, the console device's read;
  `sci_putc_polled`, which polls SCSR (`0xfffc0c`) and writes SCDR
  (`0xfffc0e`), and `console_write_polled`; `duartb_settings_done`,
  `psos_io_control` of device 0, the SCI; `sci_async_allowed_update`;
  `sci_echo_none`, the bare `rts` the set-up stores as the echo
  (`0x2e5e0`), which the port pins to `0x2dc38` where image_diff pairs
  the Z3817A's with `acia_isr`'s last instruction (`0x2d4f2`); and
  `sci_timecode_released`, which returns the byte
  `sci_release_for_timecode` sets (`0x103efb`).  `modules.tsv` files
  the SCI's routines under `devices/sci`, as the Z3805A 3543B's does, by
  hand rows, five of them in place of the Z3817A's rows, which file
  them under `devices/duartb` (`seed_names.py` lets a hand row take a
  seeded row's place).

Left under their DUART names: `duart_stop` (`0x2d582`), `shutdown`'s
last call, which writes 3 to the ACIA's control register, and
`duart_test` (`0x2d466`), which clears `selftest.uart` alone ("The link
list"); the DUART channel B settings' routines and table
(`duartb_parity_restrict`, `duartb_eight_bits_restrict`,
`duartb_settings_preset`, `duartb_settings_defaults`), which act on a
settings record of their own beside the SCI's (`duartb_settings`,
`0x1029aa`, a hand row; `sci_settings_defaults`, `0x4089e`); and the
installer's, the Z3815A's installer byte for byte.  The seeded shadow of
the DUART's write-only registers, `duart_shadow` (`0x103c94`), is
dropped (`DROPPED_58503B`): nothing refers to `0x103c94` to `0x103c97`.

Four routines start elsewhere than image_diff pairs them, and the
seeding corrects the pairings (`PORT_58503B_1_01_04`'s `corrections`):
`efc_write` is `0x2dba0`, which `efc_write_dithered` calls (`0x32d28`),
and `qspi_fpga_byte_write` `0x2dc20`, the transfer after
`qspi_fpga_word_write`'s, where image_diff pairs each with the other's;
`sci_reconfigure` is `0x2e5f6`, the I/O table's entry for
device 0's control (`0x5c060`), which opens a frame for the status
register it raises at its end (`0x2e740` to `0x2e752`), where the
Z3817A's keeps none (image_diff pairs `0x2e5fa`, the code after the
`link`); and `enter_installer` is `0x230c2`, which `scpi_task_run`
calls (image_diff pairs `0x230ee`, its `trap #11` and `rts`).  Hand
rows name what the Z3817A's rows cannot: the interpolator's and the
internal reference's self-tests and power-up checks
(`selftest_interpolator`, `selftest_internal_reference` and their
`_with_lamp` kin, `0x279ce`, `0x27a34`, `0x28f52`, `0x28f7c`), the
Z3817A having no interpolator and its internal reference's in the
bytes image_diff pairs with the interpolator's; `selftest_table`
(`0x28662`), the Z3816A's fourteen entries where the Z3817A has eleven;
`hmon_check_antenna_current` (`0x31aa6`), which the Z3817A's four
bytes pair with `ocxo_warm`; and `timecode_update` (`0x23e0c`), which
takes a block and a length where the Z3817A's takes nothing.  Hand rows
also give `sci_init` its register convention, and name
the SCI's settings, `sci_settings` (`0x1029a2`), the QSPI device's read,
`qspi_device_read` (`0x2f0f2`, the I/O table's `0x5c078`), and the SCI
session's error reports, `sci_scpi_error_reports` (`0x1038ec`), which
`sci_scpi_session_init` fills and the decompiler had written over
untyped words.

The SCI's driver state, `sci` (`0x103ee6`), is the 58503B's own
`sci_driver`, `0x1e` bytes to `qspi`, each field where the driver's
code addresses it: the Z3817A's fields to `reconfiguring`, then
`async_allowed` at `+0x14` (`sci_async_allowed_update`) and the byte
`sci_release_for_timecode` sets at `+0x15`, with no
`in_transmit_interrupt`, `transmitting` or `transmit_busy`, and the
baud constant (`sci_baud_set`), the echo at `+0x18` and the last
line's terminator (`sci_gets`) after them.

and polled-write routines name the DUART's registers and loopback

re-merge it leaves out `common/duart.c` and `common/duarta.c` too,
the DUART's input debounces and channel A driver, and
`common/scpi_unsupported.c`, the root's unsupported four-letter

the Z3817A 3752 lack (`common/scpi_output_pin1.c`,

`common/duartb_settings.c`, `common/duartb_test.c` and
`common/installer/installer_duart_transmit.c`,
every function of

the other images' copy.

The hand-written C began as the Z3817A's, copied: every file of its
`rewritten/` but `duartb.c`, whose one function, DUART channel B's
transmit interrupt, the 58503B does not have, each function in them
identical or relocated in the diff against the Z3817A, their comments'
addresses moved to the 58503B's through the diff's matches; and its
bindings.  `pps.c`'s `pps_interrupt` is the image's own: each 1PPS
the 58503B ticks the clock, sends the time code while the SCI is
released for it (`timecode_pps_send`, `0x23dea`), signals the front
panel's task as the 58503A does (`front_panel_signal_pps`) and pulses
the FPGA's seconds phase bit on an odd GPS second as the Z3815A does
(`pps_sync_pulse`, `0x4af88`), where the Z3817A calls `timecode_update`
alone (**read**, `0x22f38`).

The flash test is the Z3815A 4010's (`flash_test`,
`flash_test_erase`, `flash_test_program`, relocated in the diff against
the Z3815A, at `0x3b944`, `0x3b8a6` and `0x3b8ee`), and its C is copied from the
Z3815A's `scpi_diagnostic.c` (`rewritten/58503b-1.01.04/scpi_diagnostic.c`),
with a hand row for the RAM copy of the flash's block eraser it calls,
`flash_ram_erase_block` (`0x1045a0`, called at `0x3b8ba`).  The NV and
log routines are not copied: those the other images write by hand
are changed in every diff against them (`nv_slot_erase`,
`log_event`) or are identical but call one that does
(`clear_nv`), and the 58503B's `log_erase` matches the Z3817A's, which
writes none.  The operating system's reports are the Z3817A's copies
of the 58503A's already (`os.c`).

The QSPI's transfer set-up, `0x2ec28`, is `qspi_queue_setup`, a hand
row: the 58503A 3704's, whose cases image_diff takes for other
functions, and the Z3817A's `0x2f6d8`, which it leaves unnamed (a
lead, changed at 0.864), its 13 minor devices through a jump table
(`0x2ec60`), called by `qspi_start`, `qspi_send` and `qspi_transfer`
(`0x2eed0`, `0x2f1ee`, `0x2f280`).  `qspi_start` and `qspi_send` are
`common/qspi.c`'s, copied (`rewritten/58503b-1.01.04/qspi.c`), each
relocated in the diffs against the 58503A and the Z3817A: the
decompiler's `qspi_send` drops the interrupt mask and the status
register's restore, and its `qspi_start` reads the running flag once.

Hand rows (the names file's "row added by hand for the 58503B 1.01.04")
give the export what it needs to compile and the profile its places:
the kernel's configuration and I/O table, `psos_config` and `io_table`
(four devices: the SCI, the QSPI, the string device and one of bare
returns); `gps_response_cursor`, which `common/gps.h` names; the
numeric descriptors of `:DIAG:ROSC:EFC:DADC` and `:ASLOPe`, which
Ghidra had taken for code; `gps_alternate_status` (`0x5209a`), which

the routines `pps_interrupt` calls, with `task_fpnl`; and `sci`, the
SCI's driver state, `0x1e` bytes, two fewer than the Z3817A's

## The NV records

**Read.**  The EEPROM's two NV register records, `nv_records`, are at
`0x4000c0` and `0x400160`, `0xa0` bytes each.  `nv_slot_erase`
(`0x42a44`) erases a slot by making its tag's first byte
`nv_settings.gps_utc` with the high four bits set, unless it already
is, and `nv_restore` (`0x42a8c`) reads both tags first, slot 0's then
slot 1's (`0x42aa2`, `0x42aa4`): when neither slot loads, two erased
tags that agree give back a GPS UTC setting of 2 or 3 over the
defaults (`nv_settings_defaults`, `0x407d2`, a hand row; `0x42b18` to
`0x42b3e`).  Both are written by hand
(`rewritten/58503b-1.01.04/nv.c`): the decompiler compared the tag's
signed char with an int of `0xf0` to `0xff`, which gcc takes as never
equal, so that its copy wrote without reading, and read the two tags
as plain values, which gcc loaded in the other order.

`qspi_transfer` (`0x2f21a`) is one of five functions whose export
never showed their writes of the status register, which the
decompiler does not render; the hand-written C of each does what the
image does.  `qspi_transfer` enters supervisor mode and keeps the

#0xfcff,sr`, `0x2ebce` and `0x2ebd2`, `0x2ef14` and `0x2ef18`) and
restore it on each way out (`0x2ec1c`; `0x2ef44` and `0x2ef80`), as
`common/qspi.c`'s do.  `leap_minute_59_arm` and `leap_minute_61_arm`
(`0x239f2`, `0x239f8`) end in one tail that enters supervisor mode

`leap_minute_arm`.
