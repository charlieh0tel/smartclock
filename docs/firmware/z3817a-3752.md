# Z3817A 3752 against Z3815A 4010 and 58503A 3704

The Z3817A's image, `third_party/firmware/images/z3817a-3752.bin`
(revision 3752, `*IDN?` answering `3752-A`), read from a bench unit's
flash through its pForth console (`third_party/NOTICE`).  The Z3817A is
a timing module for a Motorola cell site, with no GPS engine of its own:
it disciplines its oscillator to one of two time references, TIMEREF1
and TIMEREF2, each a pulse and a Motorola Oncore binary stream from a
receiver elsewhere.  Its folder is `third_party/firmware/analysis/z3817a-3752/`.
The first wave seeded its names from the Z3815A 4010's, as the
Z3815A's were seeded from the 58503A's ([`z3815a-4010.md`](z3815a-4010.md)),
and read what the image does with the references; the second named its
Oncore code from the 58503A 3704, a second base, made the export and
its checks, typed the records whose layouts the export showed to be
the Z3815A's, and ran the function check, the image unfinished.

What is read from the image is marked **read**, what follows from it
**inferred**, and what a bench unit showed **bench**; "Not
established" lists what none of them settles.
Addresses are the Z3817A's unless another image is named.

The two are close; the Z3815A carries more, and its installer is the
Z3817A's byte for byte: the first 128 KiB, the boot code and the
Vatican installer's records, are the same bytes in both but for four
at `0x4000`.  The Z3817A is seeded from it
(`seed_names.py`'s `PORT_Z3817A_3752`; `make seed-names`), its masked
matches checked by their calls (image_diff's `--callee-ties`, "The
function check"), with the exceptions the matches needed, three pins
and four corrections (`PINNED_Z3817A`, `CORRECTIONS_Z3817A`; below),
and no dropped match.
Its types started as the Z3815A's `smartclock.h` and `check-layout.c`
(`rewritten/z3817a-3752/types/`); the records the export showed to be
laid out otherwise are the Z3817A's own (below, "Types"), the rest
the Z3815A's layouts, checked at this image's addresses, and the types
nothing of the Z3817A's uses are gone.

The 58503A matches the GPS task better (`cut -f1-3,7` of the two TSVs,
`applications/gps`): the Z3817A, like the 58503A, frames Oncore binary
where the Z3815A frames a Furuno's NMEA, and the Z3815A's names for its
NMEA decoders do not carry.  So the 58503A is the port's second base
(`seed_names.py`'s `second_base_folder`; the names file's header gives
the rule): a function the Z3815A's matches find new is carried from the
58503A's matches where those carry it and its name is free, with its
local rows, and so is a ROM table at an address and under a name no
other row takes, each row's note ending "from the 58503A 3704, by
image_diff as the second base".  It names the Oncore decoders
(`oncore_position_status_8_message`, `oncore_extension_message` and the
rest), `oncore_decode_fields` and the field code tables they pass it,
the message table `oncore_messages`, the start-up scripts' steps and
`pllp_task`, among others; `make seed-names` lists them under "Rows
from the second base".  The Z3815A named its SCI routines, which the
Z3805A matches, by hand rows instead.

Six of the Z3815A's pairings are corrected, each by the caller that
names the function (the names file's header): `scpi_message_done` and
`scpi_input_closed` are `0x39936` and `0x3993a`, which `scpi_interpret`
calls, and `output_state_program` and `pulse_program`, two bare `rts`
that image_diff swaps, `0x47ca2` and `0x47ca4`, which
`output_state_done` and `pulse_settings_done` call; `printf` is
`0x2f338`, which `deferred_flush` calls, where image_diff pairs
`duartb_printf`, whose code differs only in its write's offset; and
`sci_puts` is `0x2e7a2`, which `sci_write` and `sci_putc` call, where
image_diff pairs `0x2e7ac`, the code after its test for no length; and
`measured_uncertainty_compute` is `0x45dea`, which
`measured_uncertainty_update` calls, where image_diff starts it 6 bytes
early, in the `pllc_request_b` descriptor before it.  A
primary function image_diff matches inside the installer's records, the
installer's copy, has no row: `scpi_task_create` and three of the SCI
SCPI session's routines matched there, which this image's primary does
not have in that form (below, "The SCPI session").

Three cautions for anyone reading the seeded names or a Ghidra listing
made with them:

- A changed or lead row keeps the Z3815A's name for code that may do
  something else here: `0x41b94` is seeded `duartb_settings_preset`
  but copies `sci_settings_defaults` over `sci_settings` and
  reconfigures device 0, the SCI (`0x41bae`, `0x41bb4`; **read**); the
  DUART's own init is `0x2ddda`, unnamed (below).
- Records still typed with the Z3815A's `smartclock.h` (`pll` among
  them) are its layouts over the Z3817A's bytes: a field name in a
  listing says where the Z3815A keeps that field, not what the
  Z3817A's byte there is.  The notes below give addresses, not those
  names.
- Seeded names that mislead here in particular: `backp_task`
  (`0x4a2f6`) is the loop's wait for the periodic tick ("The pulse");
  `duartb_printf` writes device 0, and `drtb_release_for_timecode` (`0x2e04e`) asks
  whether channel B has a byte ("The ports"); the `puck` record's field
  names over `0x1021f4` to `0x1021fc` are the Z3815A's.

## Layout and chip selects

**Read.**  The Z3815A's layout: the boot code and the Vatican installer
in the first 128 KiB, the installer's records at `0x8000`, unpacked to
`0x100400` and entered at `0x1013a6`, the primary's vectors at
`0x20000`.

The chip selects ([`chip-selects.md`](chip-selects.md), "Z3817A 3752"):
the boot code's two runs are the Z3815A's.  The
primary's map differs from the Z3815A's primary in three places:

- **No switch word.**  CS7 stays a write-only 8-bit window at
  `0x306000`, as the boot code leaves it, where the Z3815A's primary
  makes it the 16-bit read-only switch word at `0x302000`.  Nothing in
  the image reads `0x302000`; what the Z3815A takes from the word's bit
  12 the Z3817A takes from its EEPROM (below, "The loop's constants").
- **No CS10.**  The window at `0x500000` the Z3815A opens is not
  opened.
- **Pins.**  CSPAR0 `0x2fef` and CSPAR1 `0x00ab`, where the Z3815A
  writes `0x2fff` and `0x02af`.

Port E (`0x245b8` to `0x245c8`): PEPAR `0x12`, so PE1 and PE4 are bus
pins, DSACK1 and DS, and the rest port pins; DDRE `0xc0`, PE6 and PE7
outputs, PE0, PE2, PE3 and PE5 inputs; PORTE0 starts `0xc0`.  PE6 is
the FPGA's PROGRAM line, as in the other images (`fpga_program`,
`0x326c0`).

## The time references

### TIMEREF1 or TIMEREF2: PE7

**Read.**  Two routines alone write PE7, and both call `0x52ab6`,
which calls `gps_init` and so restarts the GPS task:

- `0x4d430` toggles PE7 and the byte at `0x1026e5`, `pll` + `0x4b`,
  calls `0x52ab6` and logs "Switched gps ref".  `0x4d4b0`, a
  once-a-pass check of the loop's, calls it in power-up with no good
  reference, or in holdover, when a flag `0x4d6ca` sets is set
  (`:DIAGnostic:REFerence:CPORt`'s action, below) or a
  counter (`pll` + `0x46`) has passed 300 (in holdover also with
  holdover longer than 300 s).  The counter counts seconds: 1 a pass
  in 1PPS mode and 2 in PP2S mode, whose passes are 2 s apart (below,
  "The pulse").
- `0x4d46e` makes PE7 agree with the byte: high while it is 0, low
  while it is 1; `pll_restart` (`0x4ca66`) and `0x4cb34` call it.

The status screen (`status_screen`, `0x50ede`) prints "to TIMEREF1" or
"to TIMEREF2" by the snapshot's byte at `+0x121`, and the stage text
(`0x50b0e`) ": TIMEREF1 invalid" while it is 0, ": TIMEREF2 invalid"
otherwise.  The snapshot's byte is a copy of `pll` + `0x4b`
(`status_snapshot`, `0x50d66`), and `:DIAGnostic:REFerence:STATus?`
(`0x3cafa`) answers 1 for it 0 and 2 otherwise.  So by the firmware's
own text, byte 0 and PE7 high is TIMEREF1.

The bench unit's own log agrees: its timing from its last power-on to
"GPS reference valid", with VALID and a 1 PPS on TIMEREF2 alone, is a
start on TIMEREF1 and a switch at the 300th pass.
So PE7 high selects TIMEREF1 (**read**, and the bench's log).

**Inferred.**  PE7 drives a selector between the two references, and
the byte is which one is selected.  Since a switch restarts the GPS task, the selector
carries each reference's Oncore stream as well as its pulse.  The
firmware hunts: with no reference good it switches every 300 s or so
until one is.

### The valid line: PE5

**Read.**  `gps_alternate_status` (`0x51d64`) returns
`porte_read_steady(0x20)` (`0x33670`): PE5, read until ten reads in a
row agree, or 1000 reads, after which the last is taken; 1 while PE5
is high.  `status_snapshot` (`0x50d7c`) and `gpsm_task` (`0x5300a`)
call it, as they call the Z3815A's `gps_alternate_status`, which
returns 1, for the external reference's status
(`gps_receiver_status_update`).  `porte_read_steady` is the 58503A's
byte for byte, where nothing calls it (its `names.tsv` row); here
`gps_alternate_status` does.

From there, end to end:

- **The external record.**  `gps_receiver_status_update(1, PE5)`
  (`0x51946`) takes PE5 high as its one condition, assesses 1 s and
  makes the external record (`gps` + `0x560`) valid; PE5 low makes it
  invalid, or questionable for its hysteresis (`+0x572`,
  `ext_reference_hysteresis_set`, `0x5192a`).
- **The reference.**  `gps_reference_update` (`0x4a13a`) makes
  `gps_reference_good` (`0x102a99`) while the external record or the
  GPS record is valid and the 1 PPS dead flag (`0x10203a`) is clear.
  Questionable counts only for the GPS record, in n3 lock pll and
  startup pll: an external record that leaves valid ends the good
  reference at the next reading.  Each change raises the satellites'
  condition, set or cleared (`status_happening`).
- **The loop.**  `gps_reference_good`'s readers are the stages
  (`holdover` `0x48b58`, `holdover_recovery` `0x49126`, `startup_pll`
  `0x493da`, `pll_normal` `0x49b1e`, `powerup` `0x4b3d6`),
  `pll_reference_check` (`0x4b6f6`), the reference check `0x4d4b0`
  (its hunt, above, and the mode switch, below), `efc_calibration_measure`
  (`0x4da40`) and `pll_init`.  The external record's own readers are
  `gps_reference_update`, `status_snapshot` (`0x50dd2` to `0x50de4`)
  and `:DIAGnostic:REFerence:STATus?` (`0x3cb20`, below).  No FFOM,
  TFOM or time code routine reads PE5 or the record: they see it only
  through the stages.
- **What shows.**  The status screen's ACQUISITION bracket drops the
  `(GPS) ` prefix while the external record is valid ("Two ways to a
  good reference", below).
- **A switch of reference.**  `0x4d430`'s restart of the GPS task
  (`gps_init`, `0x52a16`) clears the whole `gps` record, `0x5c0` bytes
  (`0x52a26`), the external record with it, which then assesses its
  1 s again.

**Inferred.**  PE5 is the board's VALID, asserted high: the bench's
VALID, a jumper to +5 V, made the record valid ("Two ways to a good
reference").  The external record is re-assessed at every switch, as if
PE5 changed meaning with PE7, and the hunt only makes sense if it does:
PE5 is then the selected reference's VALID, asserted high, through the
same selector as the pulse.  The brief this wave
started from named PE4 as VALID; PE4 is a bus pin here (PEPAR bit 4,
DS).

### The pulse: 1PPS or PP2S, counted on PAI

**Read.**  `gps_pps_check` (`0x33d08`) counts the reference's pulses
on the GPT's pulse accumulator, as the other images count the GPS
engine's 1PPS (`counter.md`, "Which 1 PPS edges"): PACTL `0x50`, PAEN
and PEDGE, rising edges.  It keeps two verdicts, each logged as it
changes: the byte at `0x10204b`, "Ref 1pps signal is dead", "is ok"
and "frequency is incorrect", and `0x10204c` the same for "Ref pp2s".
Each check wants 1 to 3 pulses since the last check for the 1PPS and
since the one before for the PP2S; a gate counter, 2 a check, ends the
long gates, which want 8 to 12 pulses at 10 and 17 to 23 at 20 for the
1PPS, and 8 to 12 at 20 for the PP2S.  The GPS's 1PPS dead flag
the rest of the firmware reads is the 1PPS verdict or the PP2S's, by
the mode below.

`0x4d4b0` picks the mode: with no good reference for more than 60 s
(its counter, above), it switches to PP2S when the 1PPS is dead and
the PP2S alive ("Switched to PP2S mode") and back when the reverse
holds ("Switched to 1PPS mode"), each setting a mode byte and a second
byte, 5 in PP2S and 10 in 1PPS.

**Read.**  The two bytes and what reads them:

- **The mode**, `pll` + `0x3ea` (`0x102a84`): 0 for 1PPS, 1 for PP2S,
  cleared by `pll_init`'s clear of `pll` (`0x4c9ae`, `0x3ee` bytes).
  It picks the dead flag's verdict (`0x33cf2`, `0x34124`), is the
  snapshot's `+0x122` (`0x50d6c`) and the second answer of
  `:DIAGnostic:REFerence:STATus?` (1 or 2, `0x3cb0e`).  Where the loop
  waits for a reading with the pulse dead, on the periodic interrupt's
  tick (event `0x200`, `0x4a2f6`, called from `0x4a338` while
  `0x10203a` or `0x10203b` is set), it waits two ticks in PP2S.
  `coarse` halves its frequency estimate, the difference of two
  readings, in PP2S (`0x4a5f6`); `fine` takes 25 readings instead of 50
  for its fit (`0x4a81e`) and counts each as 2 s (`0x4a860`,
  `0x4aaf0`).  `0x4d4b0`'s own counters add 2 a pass in PP2S, 1 in
  1PPS (`0x4d4ec`, `0x4d532`, `0x4d5b4`).  `watchdog_report`
  (`0x33a04`) lets the PLL task's count be zero in one report of two
  in PP2S (`0x33a3e`).
- **The block's length**, `pll` + `0x3ec` (`0x102a86`): the readings to
  a block, 10 from `pll_init` (`0x4ca20`).  `holdover` (`0x48d86`,
  `0x48fe6`), `startup_pll` (`0x49638`) and `pll_normal` (`0x49dca`)
  count readings to it where the Z3815A's count to the immediate 10
  (`pll_normal`, Z3815A `0x4e0a6`; `PLL_STAGES_BLOCK_SECONDS` in its
  `pll_stages.c`); `startup_pll` (`0x499c2`) and `pll_normal`
  (`0x49f18`) multiply the integral's step by it where the Z3815A's
  multiply by 10.0 (`PLL_STAGES_BLOCK_LENGTH`).  The time constant's
  terms keep 10.0 (`pll_normal`'s ramped time constant, `pll` + `0x3c8`,
  less 10, and its aging term times 10).

The same pass switches TIMEREF before it looks at the mode: with the
pass counter past 300 in power-up and no good reference, `0x4d430`
runs and clears the counter (`0x4d61c` to `0x4d65a`), so the mode
check, which wants the counter past 60, waits for the next one.  The
check clears the counter only at a switch of TIMEREF (whether a restart
of the loop clears it is not traced), so once a reference has been
taken for 300 passes, losing it to a pulse of the other kind switches
TIMEREF first.

**Inferred.**  A pass of the loop is one reading, one reference pulse:
2 s in PP2S.  Every reader above counts so, and a block stays 10 s, 5
readings of 2 s.  The integral's step, multiplied by 5 where the
Z3815A multiplies a 10 s block by 10, is half as large per block in
PP2S as in 1PPS: whether that halving of the integral gain is meant is not
established.  The checks of `gps_pps_check` are two seconds apart: two
1PPS pulses or one PP2S pulse a check fit both the short gates and the
long ones.

### Into the FPGA

The image loads its bitstream as the others do (`fpga_setup`,
`fpga_program`).  What the reference pulse does inside it, the count,
its start and stop and the status bits, is the FPGA trace's,
[`z3817a-trace.md`](z3817a-trace.md); what the firmware makes of them
is below, "The time interval".

## The time interval

### One reading

**Read.**  Once a pass, `interval_next_reading` (`0x4a338`) or
`interval_wait_reading` (`0x4a490`) calls `counter_read` (`0x45810`).
It waits for status bit 1, the start, and then bit 0, the stop
(`counter_wait_ready`, `0x45410`, given 2 and then 1), reads the
count's bytes at `0x18`, `0x16`, `0x14` and `0x12` (`counter_read_raw`,
`0x456fc`), takes off the preset `0x55555555` in 64 bits
(`counter_offset_apply`, `0x456d0`, the constant at `0x45a92`), and
computes, in double (`0x45852` to `0x458b0`):

    reading = count × 1.0e-7 + 5.0e-8 − 0.4000003 + :DIAGnostic:TOFFset

- `1.0e-7` and `5.0e-8` are immediates (`0x3e7ad7f29abcaf48`,
  `0x3e6ad7f29abcaf48`), multiplied and added in place
  (`double_multiply_in_place`, `0x6677e`; `double_add_in_place`,
  `0x654d0`).
- `0.4000003` is the double at `counter_configuration` + 4
  (`0x102500`), subtracted (`double_subtract_in_place`, `0x6547a`).
  `counter_configuration_init` stores it (`0x459bc`, `0x3fd9999a`,
  `0xdbb8f8da`), as the Z3805A's does (`counter.md`, "The 0.4000003 s
  calibration"); nothing else writes it.
- `:DIAGnostic:TOFFset` is the float at `0x10221a`, converted
  (`float_to_double`, `0x660bc`) and added (below, "The time offset").
- The tag is `0xfff6` (the word at `0x45a48`), and `valid` is set.

No other term enters: no interpolator term (`interpolator_read`,
`0x4574c`, has no caller and no copy of its address in the image) and
no delay.  `interval_next_reading` hands the loop the reading as a
float (`double_to_float`).  The count is an integer, so with the offset
at 0 every reading is (count − 4,000,003) × 100 ns + 50 ns: on a grid
of 100 ns, offset by half a tick, …, −150, −50, +50, +150 ns, ….

### The mean: `:SYNChronization:TINTerval?` and the status screen

**Read.**  `:SOURce:SYNChronization:TINTerval?` and
`:SOURce:PTIMe:TINTerval?` have one handler (`0x40bee`,
`handlers.txt`): it answers `interval_mean` (`0x102a88`), a float,
with the tag `0xfff6`, while `interval_mean_flag` (`0x102a8c`) is set,
and fails with data corrupt or stale otherwise.  `status_snapshot`
copies the two (`0x50c9c`, `0x50c9e`), and `status_screen_tfom`
(`0x50890`) prints `1PPS TI`, the mean by `format_si` with the unit
`s`, then `rel to TIMEREF` and 1 or 2 by the snapshot's `+0x121`
("TIMEREF1 or TIMEREF2"), or `--` while the flag is clear.
`:DIAGnostic:PTIMe:TINTerval?` (`0x3bc2a`) answers the last single
reading instead, `interval_reading`'s value as a float.

`record_access.py` over `interval_mean` finds four writers:

- `startup_pll` (`0x49696`) and `pll_normal` (`0x49e22`), at the end of
  each block: the sum of the block's readings over their number.  A
  block is `pll` + `0x3ec` passes, 10 in 1PPS mode and 5 in PP2S ("The
  pulse").  A pass adds its reading only while the reference is good
  and not questionable and the reading's magnitude is under the limit
  at `0x1023f2`, or the override at `0x1023f6` is set; so a mean is of
  10 or 5 readings at most, fewer when one fails or is refused, and a
  block with none clears the flag.
- `holdover` (`0x49050`), at the end of each block likewise, but with
  no limit on the readings and only when every pass of the block gave
  one with the reference good.
- `fine` (`0x4aada`), power-up's fine adjustment: its fit's sum of
  readings (`0x102680`) over its count, once at the fit's end.

`fine_sync_adjust` and `sync_perform` clear the flag when they step or
sync ("The sync field", below).  Nothing filters the mean before the
query reads it: `pll_normal` filters it afterward, into `pll` + `0x3c0`
with the weight 29.75 / τ, for the EFC alone.

**Inferred.**  The 10 ns steps follow from the grid.  A mean of N
readings of 50 ns + kᵢ × 100 ns is 50 ns + (Σkᵢ / N) × 100 ns:

| Mode | N | The mean's steps | Values, with the offset at 0 |
| ---- | -: | ---------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 1PPS | 10 | 10 ns | every multiple of 10 ns |
| PP2S | 5 | 20 ns | odd multiples of 10 ns only: …, −50, −30, −10, +10, … |

A mean of −40 ns, for instance, is nine readings of −50 ns and one of
+50 ns, and −30 ns eight and two.  Means on even multiples of 10 ns fit
1PPS mode, the mode the bench unit's status screen showed on
2026-11-09 ("Two ways to a good reference").  That the mean moves at all says the readings of one
block differ: the reference edge lies close enough to a clock edge
that its jitter carries it across from one reading to the next.  A
block with a reading refused or failed is a mean of 9 (or 4), whose
steps, 100 / 9 ns, leave the 10 ns grid.

### The time offset

**Read.**  The word at `0x10221a` is `:DIAGnostic:TOFFset`'s value, a
float: `counter_read` converts it with `float_to_double`, as the
Z3805A's adds its own (`counter.md`, "The reading").
`record_access.py` over it, and the image's copies of the address
(`0x41758`, `0x4193c`, `0x41982`, `0x44cf0`, `0x458a4`), give its
accesses:

- `calibration_block_read` (`0x41910`) copies the calibration block's
  `+6`, EEPROM `0x400086`, when the block's checksum holds (`0x41938`),
  and clears it otherwise (`0x41980`).
- `:DIAGnostic:TOFFset`'s descriptor (`scpi_param_DIAGnostic_TOFFset`,
  `0x44cda`) points at it: limits −3.0e-7 and +3.0e-7 (the floats at
  `0x44cca` and `0x44cce`), step 1.0e-10 (`0x44cd2`), and the done hook
  `time_offset_done` (`0x4ae14`), whose `save_calibration_block`
  (`0x4173c`) writes it back to the block (`0x41756`).
- `counter_read` reads it (`0x458a2`).

So it is seconds, set by `:DIAGnostic:TOFFset` and kept in the EEPROM,
and nothing in the firmware computes it.  The bench unit's EEPROM image
(under `images/eeprom/`, its bytes `0x80` to
`0xbf`) holds a block whose checksum holds (`record_checksum`,
`0x41314`: the byte sum of `+2` on, then its complement, `0x2a` and
`0xd5`, the block's first word) and 0.0 at `+6`.

**Inferred.**  The FPGA trace gives the count as

    count + φ = 4,000,004 + (t_internal − t_ref) / 100 ns

with φ, 0 to 1, the time from the reference edge to the next clock
edge in ticks.  With the firmware's constants:

    reading = (t_internal − t_ref) + 100 ns + (0.5 − φ) × 100 ns + TOFFset

The half tick centers the quantization: (0.5 − φ) × 100 ns runs from
−50 to +50 ns, 0 on average over φ.  But 0.4000003 s is 4,000,003
ticks, the Z3805A's offset with its reference bit High; the Z3817A's
design counts as the Z3805A's does with the bit Low, 4,000,004
(`z3805a-trace.md`, "Start, stop and status bits"), and the firmware
kept the Z3805A's constant.  So a reading is one tick, 100 ns, larger
than the interval on average.  `:DIAGnostic:TOFFset`'s range covers
−100 ns, but nothing sets it but the command and the EEPROM, and the
bench unit's block holds 0: there nothing absorbs the tick, and
whatever mean the loop holds, the internal 1 PPS sits 100 ns earlier
against the reference than the firmware's arithmetic assumes.  The
FPGA's figures are the model's nominal timing, and the board's own
delay from the connector to P82 is not in them.

### P84, register 4 and the status bits

**Read.**

- **Register 4**, the start's select, is written only by `0x4538c`
  (`fpga_set_bit` of the field table's entry 3, register 4's bit 0),
  whose callers, `pll_restart` (`0x4cacc`) and `pllp_task`
  (`0x4ccd8`), pass 0 and then call `0x4d46e`, which sets PE7 by the
  selected reference ("TIMEREF1 or TIMEREF2").  No other call of
  `fpga_set_bit` or `fpga_set_field` names entry 3 or entry 4 (the
  table's `(1, 0x01)`), and the register loads (`counter_arm`,
  `counter_load_registers`) write registers 0 to 3.  So the count
  always starts on P82, and a switch of reference (`0x4d430`) moves PE7
  alone.
- **The P84 counter.**  `0x4539e` reads read address 1 (the field
  table's entry 4's register byte, `0x45d8a`), whose high nibble is the
  P84 counter, until two reads agree, up to 20 more pairs; nothing calls
  it, and the image holds no copy of its address.  The P84 count is
  never read.
- **Read address 0.**  `fpga_read` (`0x451dc`) writes the read address
  into register 1 and reads the port.  Its callers that read address 0
  are `counter_wait_ready`, which tests bit 1 and then bit 0, and
  `0x4526c`, which takes the high nibble, the seconds count, from two
  reads that agree (called by `pllp_task` at `0x4cf56`, into `pll` +
  `0x3e4`, and by `0x4b7f6`).  `0x45246`, a test of a bit at any
  address, and `0x452d0`, a dump of the 32 addresses, have no caller.
  Nothing tests bits 2 and 3, the FPGA trace's FI.X and GJ.X.

**Inferred.**  The firmware uses neither the second 1 PPS input nor
its counter: a board that switches the references ahead of P82, by
PE7, is what it is written for.

### The sync field

**Read.**  `counter_set_sync` (`0x4557a`) writes register 3 twice, its
two low bits 0 and then the kind, the other bits kept from the image at
`0x1024fa`.  Its one caller is `counter_arm` (`0x457ea`), between
setting the preset and clearing it, while a sync is pending:
`counter_configuration` + `0xe` and + `0xf` both 2, as `sync_request`
(`0x4555a`) leaves them, with the kind at + `0x10` and a count at
+ `0x12` that each arm takes 1 from, ending the pending at 0.  So a
request for n writes the kind at each of the next n arms, one a pass;
nothing writes the field back to 0 after.  The kinds and their senders:

| Kind | Sent by |
| ---- | ------- |
| 1 | `fine_sync_adjust` (`0x4a1b8`) for a reading of +100 ns to +300 ns, reading / 100 ns arms (truncated); at +300 ns or more one arm, and its result 4 |
| 2 | the same for a negative reading |
| 3 | `sync_perform` (`0x4a292`), when the reading's magnitude passes the limit at `0x1023ee` or a sync was asked for; `coarse`'s start (`interval_next_reading` with 2, `interval_rearm(INTERVAL_ARM_SYNC)`); `efc_calibration_measure` (`0x4da40`), at its start and each point; the console's `sync_imm` (`0x2afce`) |
| any | the console's `clk_synch` (`diag_clk_synch`, `0x2afba`), its two arguments passed on |

`fine` (`0x4a6f2`) alone calls `fine_sync_adjust` and `sync_perform`,
in power-up and in its slew; `startup_pll`, `pll_normal` and `holdover`
neither step nor sync.  The writer, its caller and the kinds are the
Z3805A's (`counter.md`, "The sync field").

**Inferred.**  By the FPGA trace's reading of the field ("The sync
field" there), kind 3 restarts the divider on the selected 1 PPS and
kinds 1 and 2 step the internal 1 PPS a tick, as in the Z3805A; the
write of 0 before the kind is what makes each arm's write a change from
0, which the Z3805A's design takes as a sync pending.  The Z3817A
design's steps are not simulated (that trace's open item).

### `:DIAGnostic:REFerence` and `:DIAGnostic:TCODe`

**Read.**  `:DIAGnostic:REFerence:STATus?` (`0x3cafa`) fills five
response items (`scpi_items_DIAGnostic_REFerence_STATus`, `0x641da`:
five and the list's end), two `scpi_format_unsigned_short` and three
`scpi_format_boolean`, so it answers `+r,+m,e,g,p`:

| Item | Value | From |
| ---- | ----- | ---- |
| r | 1 TIMEREF1, 2 TIMEREF2 | the selected reference, `pll` + `0x4b` (`0x1026e5`) 0 or not |
| m | 1 1PPS, 2 PP2S | the pulse's mode, `pll` + `0x3ea` (`0x102a84`) |
| e | 1 while the external record is valid | `gps` + `0x560` (`0x1013c6`), its validity, 1; questionable (2) answers 0 |
| g | 1 while the GPS record is valid | `gps` + `0x54a` (`0x1013b0`), likewise |
| p | 1 while the pulse is alive | the 1 PPS dead flag (`0x10203a`) clear |

None has a unit: codes and flags.  The record's first byte is its
validity, as the Z3815A's `gps_reference_record.valid`, which
`gps_receiver_status_update` writes at its end here (`0x51d1a` to
`0x51d38`).

`:DIAGnostic:REFerence:CPORt`, a setter with no query (`0x3cad4`),
posts the action at `0x45de4`, `0x4d6ca`, to the loop task: in holdover,
or in power-up with no good reference, it sets `pll` + `0x4d`, on
which `0x4d4b0` switches reference at its next pass ("TIMEREF1 or
TIMEREF2"); otherwise it raises -221, "Settings conflict"
(`status_report_from(8)`).

`:DIAGnostic:TCODe`'s four masks are bytes at `0x103f00` to
`0x103f03`, ERRor's AND and OR and STATus's AND and OR; `timecode_init`
(`0x23fb0`) sets the AND masks to `0xff` and clears the OR masks, and
each setter stores its argument's low byte and each query returns it
(`0x3c946` to `0x3c9e4`).  `record_access.py` finds no other access, and
the image holds each address only in those routines.

**Inferred.**  The masks do nothing here: the Z3815A applies them to
its ES message's status and error bytes, which this image does not
build (`../scpi/undocumented.md`, `:DIAGnostic:TCODe`).

**On the bench, 2026-11-11.**  TIMEREF2 with VALID and a 1 PPS and no
stream, `:DIAGnostic:REFerence:STATus?` answered `+2,+1,1,0,1`, as
predicted: TIMEREF2, 1PPS, the external record valid, the GPS record
not, the pulse alive.

## The Oncore link

**Read.**  DUART channel A is the Oncore port.  `0x2ddda`, which
`primary_start` calls (`0x22f3a`), sets channel A to MR1A `0x13` and
MR2A `0x07` (8 bits, no parity, 1 stop), CSRA `0xbb` with ACR `0xa0`
(baud set 2: 9600), and channel B to the same framing at CSRB `0xcc`
(19200), both with their receive interrupts (IMR `0x22`).  `0x2dc7a`,
which nothing calls, sets channel A alone the same way: `duart_init`,
the Z3815A's channel B device init without channel B or its tokens.  Channel B is
the oscillator's link, below, "DUART channel B".

DUART channel B carries the oscillator module's frames (below, "The
puck").  Its receive interrupt (`0x2d8f6`) fills a ring (`0x1036f7`)
and signals `duartb_task`, which root_task makes `task_hmon`
(`0x2331a`); `duartb_receive_ready` and `duartb_getc` (`0x2e04e`,
`0x2e064`) read it, and `puck_frame_receive` (`0x3374c`) takes the
module's frames from them (**read**).  Channel B is no I/O device: its
transmit side is the DUART's own send (`0x2dfb6`) and
`duartb_transmit_interrupt` (**read**).

The message table is at `0x575d8` (`oncore/z3817a-3752.txt`, `make
docs`): the 58503A 3704's, eight-channel `@@Ea`, `@@Ek`, `@@En` and
`@@Fa` among them, and one more, `@@Sz` (entry `0x36`, below).  The `@@Ea` decoder (`0x569c2`, entry `0x32`, 76 bytes)
unpacks the message, sets the eight-channel bit and updates the channel
count, as the 58503A's (`gps.md`, "Six or eight channels").

The image has a transmitter's code on channel A too: `gpsm_task`
builds a request with `gps_request_build` and sends it with
`drta_send_message` (`0x2d1aa`, called at `0x52e4c`), and it has
start-up scripts like the 58503A's (`oncore/z3817a-3752.txt`, scripts
`0x53` and `0x54`).  But it refuses its own requests: of what the loop, the
pForth console and SCPI post, it takes nine console steps alone, none
of which transmits, and sends nothing ("What it sends").  What it takes unasked and what a
feed must give it are below, "Feeding it".

### `@@Sz`

**Read.**  Entry `0x36`: kind 2, sent by the engine only, 7 bytes, so
`@@Sz`, its checksum and the line end with no data; its decoder
(`0x56ce8`) stores 1 through the entry's record pointer, `0x10138a`,
`gps` + `0x524`, and does nothing else.  Nothing else touches the byte:
no absolute operand names it, the table's pointer at `0x582a4` is the
image's only copy of the address, and `record_access.py` over `gps`
(`0x100e66`, `0x700` bytes) finds no access at `+0x524` through a
register or an index and no pushed address in reach of it.  Only
`gps_init`'s clear of the record (`0x52a26`) writes it, back to 0.  The
58503A 3704's table has no `@@Sz`.

The UT Plus Oncore's list of binary messages gives `@@Sz` as "Power-on
Failure Message" (`third_party/motorola/ut-engg-notes.pdf`, Oncore
Technical Application Note, revision 1.0, p. 11 of 16); its format is
the Oncore User's Guide's, chapter 6, which the repository does not
hold.  The VP reference (`VPCommands.pdf`) does not list it.

**Inferred.**  An engine that reports a failed power-on self-test is
recorded and ignored: `@@Sz` touches no health check, alarm, status
bit or the GPS record's qualification.

## The ports

### SCPI and the console: the SCI

**Read.**  The I/O table (`0x59286`, three devices of six `jmp` stubs)
gives device 0 the SCI's routines: initialization `0x2ee64`
(`sci_init`), open `0x2edbc`, close `0x2ecce`, read `0x2f25e`
(`sci_read`), write `0x2f210` (`sci_write`), control `0x2f0b8`
(`sci_reconfigure`), the Z3805A's SCI routines; device 1 the QSPI,
with a read the Z3805A's has not (`qspi_device_read`, `0x2fb2e`); device 2 the string device (read `0x2ae42`,
`console_read_from_string`), which writes through `sci_write` too.
`console_init` (`0x23146`) points the string device at `0 !iodev`
(`0x28c66`) and starts the console on it, so the console moves to
device 0 (`console.md`, "the console's port is pSOS device 0"), and
makes `sci_scpi_task_start` its halt hook.  The SCPI port is device 0
as well: `scpi_port_open` (`0x2f388`) opens it and the SCPI input loop
reads it (`console_port_read`, `0x39732`, through `console_read`,
`0x2f35e`).  `root_task` starts the console at power-up while the byte
at `0x10249b` is set (`0x2336e`), else the SCPI task (`0x230c6`).  The
periodic status screen `gpsm_task` prints (`0x5306c`) goes to
device 0 too: its writer, seeded `duartb_printf` (`0x2f338`), calls
`console_write` here, not device 3.

**Inferred.**  SCPI and the pForth console share the SCI, one at a
time, as SCPI and the console share channel B in the 58503A
(`console.md`); `halt` leaves the console for SCPI.

### DUART channel B: the oscillator

**Read.**  Channel B, at 19200 baud 8N1 (above), has no device in the
I/O table.  Its receive interrupt (`0x2d8f6`, from the DUART's handler,
vector 67, `0x2da38`) puts each byte in a ring (`0x1036f7`) and
signals SIG_SIQ to the task at `0x103ec2`, which `root_task` sets to
`hmon`'s (`0x2331a`).  Each `hmon_task` pass (`0x34332`) calls
`0x3374c`, which takes the ring's bytes (`0x2e04e`, seeded
`drtb_release_for_timecode`, is "a byte is waiting"; `0x2e064` takes
it) and frames them: a `0x55` starts a frame at `0x1021f4`, six bytes
in all, and the sixth must be the sum of the first five.  A good frame
is decoded (`0x3355e`): bytes 1 to 3 a 24-bit reading (`0x102046`),
compared with the EFC's expected reading (`0x1021de`) into the EFC's
correction; byte 4's bit 0 is the oscillator's ready bit (`0x33542`,
`0x1021f8`).  It marks the link alive (`0x102040`) for 10 s.  Nothing
calls channel B's transmit (`0x2dfb6`).

That is the Z3815A's puck frame, a `0x55` frame of six bytes with its
24-bit EFC ADC reading and the status byte whose bit 0 is "puck warm"
(`z3815a-4010.md`, "The oscillator and references"), carried on a UART
where the Z3815A shifts it in on PE5 against PE7's clock.  The `0xa1`
frame is not taken: the framer starts a frame only at `0x55` and ends
it at six bytes (`0x337d8`, `0x33798`), where the Z3815A's also starts
one at `0xa1` and takes seven (`puck_link_clock`, Z3815A `0x3658e`).
The ready bit counts alone here: `0x33542` is the bit and the link
alive, where the Z3815A's `puck_warm` also wants 30 s of uptime.  The
Z3815A's link messages, "OCXO Communication Fail" and "Internal OCXO
Fail", are not in the image; the pForth word `puck` (`diag_puck`,
`0x2b120`) still prints "puck communication is alive" or "is not alive" and
"puck warm= %d".

`oven_warmed_up` (`0x32486`), the power-up stage's warm-up test, and
the oven channel's report (`0x3233c`, through `0x31e44`) are
`0x31e16`: with the oscillator byte (`0x10222e`, "The loop's
constants") 0, the ready bit and the link alive; otherwise uptime past
`0x102034`.  With byte 0, power-up passes
": OCXO warm-up" only on the oscillator's word.

### Failing the unit: `0x4d4b0`

**Read.**  `0x4d4b0` runs once a pass, from `pllp_task` after its stage
(`0x4cf76`), and its first part only while a latch,
`0x1026e6` (`pll` + `0x4c`), is clear.  It keeps three counters in
`pll`, each stepped 1 a pass in 1PPS and 2 in PP2S, so in seconds
("The pulse"):

- **Not ready**, `0x1026e2` (`pll` + `0x48`), only while the oscillator
  byte is 0: cleared, and `0x1026e4` (`pll` + `0x4a`, "has been ready")
  set, on a pass where `0x33542` gives ready (`0x4d4e0`); stepped
  otherwise.
- **Hardware**, `0x1026de` (`pll` + `0x44`): stepped while
  `status_hardware_condition()`, the `:STATus:OPERation:HARDware`
  condition, has a bit of `0x70cf`, cleared when it has none
  (`0x4d524` to `0x4d544`), whatever the oscillator byte.
- The hunt's counter, `pll` + `0x46` ("TIMEREF1 or TIMEREF2"),
  outside the latch.

With byte 0 the osc code is 1 while the link is not alive
(`0x102040` clear), else 2 when the not-ready counter is past 1800,
else 3 when the oscillator has been ready and the counter is past 300
(`0x4d4fc` to `0x4d522`).  The unit fails when the osc code is not 0
or the hardware counter has reached 300 (`0x4d548`).  Failing:

- sets the latch, so the check runs once (`0x4d554`);
- logs "Unit failed, OSR HW code: %d, osc code: %d" to the diagnostic
  log (`log_write`), the HW code the whole hardware condition word,
  not masked, and the osc code 0 when only the hardware counter
  failed it;
- sets the EFC to `0xffff0` (`0x102396`) in user mode
  (`diag_efc_mode`, `0x102394`, 1; `:DIAGnostic:ROSCillator:EFControl:MODE?`
  answers `USER`: its handler, `0x3be1a`, gives 2 for mode 1, 1 for
  mode 2 and 0 otherwise, an index into the choices `NORM`, `TIM` and
  `USER` at `0x5b69a`), with hold cause `0x100` (the word at `pll` + `0x10`) and
  `pll` + `0x14` set, as `efc_mode_done` (`0x4af64`) does for a user
  EFC: `efc_write_dithered` (`0x33850`) then writes `0xffff0 >> 4`,
  `0xffff`, to the DAC each time;
- calls `efc_clamp` with 1048560.0, which is its limit, so it logs
  "EFC at limit" and raises hardware bit 7 (`0x338fa` on; its return
  is dropped), and `hmon_check_efc` raises bit 6, "EFC near end of
  range", five checks later (`0x3395a`);
- sets the byte at `0x1023b6` (`0x4d5a4`), which `:LED:ALARm?` and
  `:LED:ALARm:MAJor?` answer (`0x3e6e8`) and for which `0x3421c` sets
  bit 2 of the lamp word (`0x1021db`, `0x34242`) that `0x236c8` writes
  to the latch at `0x300000` ("The lamps", below).  Nothing else
  writes it: no status summary drives an alarm byte here, as
  `status_summary_update` does the Z3805A's (`outputs.md`, "The
  Z3805A's lamps").

The hardware register's bits in this image (the conditions table
`status_init` gives `status_create`, `0x4030e`, conditions `0x38` to
`0x59`) are, to bit 12, those `smartclock.h`'s `status_condition_code`
names, copied from the Z3815A, with bit 5 raised by nothing; and two
more, bits 13 and 14, which the health monitor's channels named "12B"
and "-12B" raise and clear (conditions `0x56` to `0x59`, their codes at
`0x32564` and `0x3258e`; "The health monitor's channels", below).
`0x70cf` is bits 0 to 3, 6, 7 and 12 to 14:
self-test failure, the +15 V, −15 V and +5 V supplies, the EFC near
and at full scale, internal reference failure, and the two "12B"
channels; not the oven supply (4), the GPS's 1 PPS and GPS failure (8,
9) or the two event bits (10, 11).

Nothing undoes a failure but a start.  The EFC stays in user mode
until `:DIAGnostic:ROSCillator:EFControl:MODE` is set back
(`efc_mode_expire`, `0x4afb2`, expires only the timed mode), and
power-up does not advance while hold cause `0x100` is set
(`0x4b4e8`, `0x4b6b0`, mask `0x160`).  A cold start clears all of it: `pll_init`
clears the latch and counters, the defaults' copy the alarm byte
(`0x41452`, 0), the ROM copy the EFC mode (`0x41430`, 0).  A warm
start (`restart.md`) restores `pll` and the block at `0x1023b4` from
the preserved region (`0x22dda` to `0x22e2e`), latch, hold cause and
alarm byte with them, and copies the EFC mode afresh from ROM
(`0x22d4e`), normal.

**A warm start after a failure (read).**  The failure leaves the loop
in power-up, and `pllp_task` (`0x4cb34`) keeps power-up as it is where
it makes any other restored stage power-up recovery, so the power-up
self-tests run again (below) and `powerup` starts afresh: its first
call clears hold causes `0x9e` (`0x4b43c`, mask `0xff61`) and keeps
`0x100`.  Its wait for a reference (sub-state 3, `0x4b4e8`) wants
`0x160` clear, so it waits there.  The latch is set, so `0x4d4b0`
checks nothing again.  With the mode normal, `efc_write_dithered`
(`0x33850`) writes the loop's EFC (`0x1021e6`) again, not `0xffff0`;
`efc_clamp_reset`, at `hmon_task`'s start, clears the clamp's state.
The alarm byte stays set, and with it `:LED:ALARm?` and the lamp
("The lamps", below).

**Inferred.**  After a warm start the unit stays failed: in power-up,
its alarm lit, its EFC back on the loop's word and
`:DIAGnostic:ROSCillator:EFControl:MODE?` answering `NORM`, though the
cause the user mode set remains.  What clears the cause short of a
cold start: setting `:DIAGnostic:ROSCillator:EFControl:MODE` to `NORM`,
whose done hook, `efc_mode_done` (`0x4af64`), clears it in normal mode,
after which power-up can go on; but the alarm byte stays set and the
latch keeps the check off until a cold start.  The console word
`pll_restart` (`diag_pll_restart`, `0x2b566`, the only writer of the
flag `0x102a8e`) makes `pllp_task` call `pll_restart`, whose
`pll_init` clears the latch, counters and causes, but not the alarm
byte or the EFC mode.  Neither has been sent.

**The link's grant.**  `system_init` calls `pps_monitors_reset`
(`0x22ee2`), which marks the link alive and its end at 10 s of uptime
on (`0x34598`, `0x345aa`).  `0x3374c`, on each `hmon` pass and each
EFC write, clears it once uptime is past that end (`0x33768` to
`0x33774`), and a good frame renews it for 10 s (`0x337c0`).

**The first pass (read).**  `pllp_task`'s start, on a cold start or a
warm one in power-up, runs `counter_reset`, then the power-up
self-tests (`selftest_print_failure`, `0x2864a`; its table, `0x286fa`:
DISP, PROC, RAM, EEPROM, UART, QSPI, FPGA, INT_REF, GPS, POWER), and
only then its loop.  DISP is `lamp_test` (`0x2854a`), five steps of
`0x133` ticks; INT_REF (`0x28500`) pauses `0x400` ticks; GPS
(`0x4bbe4`) posts step `0x51` and then script `0x56` (`Ea`, step
`0x4b`, `Ca`, `Fa`, `Cg` 1, step `0x4b`, `Ea`;
`oncore/z3817a-3752.txt`) to the GPS task, and polls for each answer
once every `0x400` ticks, up to 130 polls for the first and 30 for the
second (`0x4bc44`, `0x4bcf4`).  An answer of 0 takes the engine's
self-test word (`0x1011c0`) as the result; 8, the GPS task's timeout,
gives `0x20000`; no answer in time, `0x10000` (`0x4bd28` to
`0x4bd54`).  The GPS task's request check (`gps_request_admitted`,
"What it sends") admits step `0x51`, which is in its list, and refuses
script `0x56`, which is not, and `gpsm_task` answers the refusal at
once with result 0, done (`0x52bb4`, `gps_count_response`, which sends
the request back to `plla_exchange` with its result): the self-test's
poll of `plla_exchange` takes that answer within a poll or two and
the result is the self-test word, as for an engine that passed.  The
word has one writer besides `gps_init`'s clear: the decoder of `@@Ca`
and `@@Fa`, `word_copy_14_bits` (`0x5615e`), which `oncore_messages`'
entries `0x2a` and `0x35` (`0x575d8`, 60 bytes an entry) give with
`0x1011c0` as its destination (their first decoder's, at `+0x24`); it
stores the message's big-endian word whole.  No code writes the word
by its address or through a register: `gps_selftest_run` (`0x4bc9a`,
`0x4bd4a`) and `gps_diag_sequence` (`0x4c902`) read it (`make
record-access IMAGE=z3817a-3752 RECORD='0x100e66 0x5c0'`, offset
`0x35a`).  The script that asks for both, `0x56`, is the one the
check refuses.  The loop's first pass is power-up's start (sub-state 0,
`0x4b458`): it rearms the interval and returns without a wait, and

## The board as the image drives it

**Read**, from the image's drivers.

- *The QSPI's devices.*  `qspi_queue_setup` (`0x2f6d8`) takes devices
  5 to 19 through its table at `0x2f726`, where the Z3805A's takes 0
  to 12 (`structures.md`, "The QSPI's devices").  The EFC DAC is
  device 5, code 5 (`0x2f8b2`); the ADC device 10, two 16-bit words in
  mode 0, divider 24, the first on code `0xe` and the second, `0xe000`
  in the self-test, on code `0xa` (`0x2f744`); the FPGA's loader and
  readback devices 12, 16 and 19, code `0xc`.  Devices 15, 17 and 18
  are set up, 15 never sent.  No device sends three 12-bit words: no
  hex DAC, as no interpolator.
- *The EFC.*  `efc_write` (`0x2e67c`) sends device 5 and then clears
  `0x306000`, CS7's write window, as the Z3805A's strobe.  Device 5
  writes in SPI mode 2 and reads in mode 3 (SPCR0 `0x8204`, `0x8304`),
  where the Z3805A's EFC writes in mode 3 and reads in mode 2, and the
  self-test shifts its reading right once before comparing
  (`qspi_efc_test`, `0x288c4`), which the Z3805A's does not.
  **Inferred**: the EFC's shift register shifts on SCK's fall, where
  the Z3805A's 74HC164s shift on its rise; a read in mode 3 then comes
  a bit early, and the test's words, `0x5555` and `0x2aaa`, keep their
  top bit clear for it.
- *The ADC.*  Its table at `0x32498` names five channels, all
  supplies: 1 `5V` (4.5 to 5.5 V), 2 `15V` (13.5 to 17.5 V), 3 `-15V`,
  4 `12B` (11 to 13 V) and 5 `-12B`, the conversions the Z3805A's
  (`0x31e56` to `0x31eec`).  No oven, current or temperature channel.
  `adc_read` (`0x2e614`) sends pin (n & 7) >> 1, plus 4 for an odd n
  (`0x2e61e` to `0x2e630`), where the Z3805A's sends pin 2 x (n & 3) +
  (n >> 2).
- *The oscillator's link: DUART channel B.*  With the oscillator byte
  (EEPROM `0x40009c`, "The loop's constants") 0, `0x3374c` reads
  channel B (`drtb_get_byte`, "DRTB receiver error") for a six-byte
  report: `0x55`, 24 bits, a status byte, and the sum of the five
  before.  The status byte's bit 0 is the oscillator's ready bit
  (`0x33542`), which power-up's warm-up waits for (`0x31e16`): without
  it the loop never leaves ": OCXO warm-up".  The 24 bits are the
  oscillator's reading of its EFC:
  `0x3355e` compares them with the EFC it set times the a/d
  calibration (`0x102222`, EEPROM `0x40008c`, 16.24 on the bench unit),
  "err= %d corr= %f efc= %f adc= %d" (`0x345c8`), and integrates the
  difference into a correction of the EFC: `err` is the expected
  reading (`0x1021de`, set at each EFC write, `0x33832`) less the
  report's; from the fifth report after an EFC write (`0x1021ec`,
  `0x335b4`) `corr` (`0x1021e2`) gains `err` × 0.001 (`0x335e2`), and is
  cleared past ±500000 (`0x335fa`); `efc` (`0x1021e6`) is the EFC plus
  `corr`, and `0x33850` writes it, made an integer and clamped, to the DAC
  after each report unless the EFC is forced (`0x102394`).  The
  correction is on from start for an oscillator of kind 0 (`0x34580`)
  and off for the others; the console's `efc_comp` (`0x2b104`) sets
  it.  Ten seconds without a
  report clears `0x102040`, and the loop's check (`0x4d4b0`) fails the
  unit at once, "Unit failed, OSR HW code: %d, osc code: 1", the EFC
  set to full scale ("EFC at limit").  The same check fails it after
  300 passes of any hardware condition in `0x70cf`.
- *Self-test bits.*  `selftest_summary` (`0x277f2`) sets bit 1 for
  the CPU, 2 the RAM, 3, 4, 5 the QSPI's devices (`0x2891a`: the ADC,
  the FPGA's loader, the EFC), 6 the FPGA's counters ("FPGA: counters
  not preset", `0x45a0c`), 8 the internal reference, 9 and 10, from
  the cells at `0x100e26` on.

## The loop's constants

**Read.**  `loop.md`, "τ and G", gives where each image takes τ and
G.  The Z3817A's block of defaults is at `0x41450`, 29 bytes that
`system_init` copies to `0x1023b4` (`0x22e5e`, `0x22e94`), its float at

where in the other images `loop_time` sets `+0x14`.  `pll_init` copies
`+0x14`, `0x1023c8`, into the cell the start-up ramp steps
(`pll` + `0x3c8`), and `0x4ae46` and `0x4aeb8` keep the two in order.

**Inferred.**  `+0x14` is the ramp's first constant, the 150 s the
other images hold as an immediate (`loop.md`, "Starting up").

What the Z3815A picks by the switch word's bit 12 the Z3817A picks by
`oscillator_kind` (`0x10222e`), which `oscillator_kind_read`
(`0x418ec`) loads from the EEPROM's
`0x40009c` (0 when it is `0xff`, or when its caller asks) and
`save_calibration_block` reads:

| Byte | τ | G |
| ---- | - | - |
| 0 | 1000 s (`0x41450 + 0x18`) | +5.0 × 10⁻¹³ (`0x4180a`) |
| 1 | 500 s (`0x4ca5a`, `pll_init`) | −2.125 × 10⁻¹² (`0x41820`) |

### The update law

**Read.**  `pll_normal` (`0x49b1e`) runs the Z3815A's law (`loop.md`,
"The update, compared": its K, k and a from τ, the aging slope filtered
over the ramped time constant and clamped), with the differences
below.
Each ten-second block (the block's length, `pll` + `0x3ec`; "The
pulse") ends in:

    e  = x̄                                    no setpoint
    f ← (1 − a)·f + a·e                        a = 29.75 / τ
    v ← ((τᵣ − 10)·v + 10·x) / τᵣ              x the fit's slope, v pll + 0x3bc
    d  = clamp(v, −M, M)                       M = [0x102aae]
    I ← I + N·k·(d / (2700·k) + f)             N = pll + 0x3ec
    u  = K·f + B + I + c·s                     K = 1 / (G·τ), s = 0

- **No setpoint.**  The block's mean goes into the filter as it is
  (`0x49e22` to `0x49e62`): the Z3815A's subtracts `ti_setpoint`, the
  console word `phase_off`'s (`loop.md`'s x₀), and the Z3817A's console
  has no `phase_off` (`pforth/z3817a-3752.txt`).
- **No current term.**  The temperature correction's current is the
  constant 0.0 (`tc_coefficient_product` at `0x49f70` and on entry at
  `0x49c12`, given the frame's 0.0), where the Z3815A's is
  `adc_average(ADC_OVEN_CURRENT)`: this image has no oscillator-current
  channel ("The health monitor's channels").  So B is the EFC in force
  on entry, and c·s is 0.
- **The integral's step** is multiplied by the block's length, N, read
  at `0x49f18`, where the Z3815A's multiplies by 10.0 ("The pulse").
- τᵣ, `pll` + `0x3c8`, is set to τ on each entry (`0x49bae`), where the
  Z3815A's is left as the start-up ramp hands it over, τ either way
  after a ramp that finishes.

K = (1/G)/τ (`0x102aa6`), k = (1/G)/τ²/4 (`0x102aaa`) and a = 29.75/τ
are set on entry (`0x49bb4` to `0x49c02`), τ being `max_loop_time`
(`0x1023cc`), and each block's first second gives the loop the OCXO
calibration's gain (`efc_gain_from_calibration`), as the Z3815A's
`pll_stages_loop_gain`.  The readings summed, the hold causes of a block
with none, and the TFOM and frequency tolerance set on entry (1 × 10⁻⁶,
1 × 10⁻⁷) are the Z3815A's.

The τ and G it runs with are the pair the table above picks by the
EEPROM's byte at `0x40009c`, through `0x10222e`.

## How it differs from the Z3815A

From the two diffs and the image's code.

- **No interpolator.**  The interpolator's calibration and self-tests
  are missing (`devices/ti` in the diff), and `counter_read`
  (`0x45810`) makes a reading of the count alone: count × 100 ns plus
  50 ns, less the configuration's offset, plus `:DIAG:TOFFset`
  (**read**).  The mean of a block of such readings moves in steps of
  10 ns in 1PPS mode, which is what the bench shows, and carries a
  constant tick (**inferred**; "The time interval").
- **I/O table.**  Three devices, copied from `0x59286` to `0x102b38`

- **Handlers.**  `:DIAGnostic:TEMPerature?` (`0x3c594`) and

## The puck

**Read.**  `puck_frame_receive` (`0x3374c`), which `efc_write_dithered`
calls first (`0x33852`), reads DUART channel B's ring: a `0x55` starts a frame
(`puck.framing`), its bytes go to `puck.frame` through `puck.cursor`,
and six bytes whose first five sum to the sixth make a good frame.  For
one it calls `efc_adc_correct` (`0x3355e`) and sets `puck_alive` until
10 s on (`puck_alive_until`); once that lapses it clears `puck_alive`.
The frame is the Z3815A's `0x55` frame (`z3815a-4010.md`, "How it
differs from the Z3805A"): the EFC ADC's 24 bits from its second byte,
high first, and a status byte whose bit 0 `puck_warm` (`0x33542`)
reads.  `efc_adc_correct` keeps the reading (`efc_adc_reading`),
compares it with `puck.efc_adc_expected`, which `efc_set` (`0x33846`)
writes, and from the sixth frame on, with corrections on, adds the
difference to `puck.error_sum`, whose thousandth is `puck.correction`,
zeroed past 500000, writing the EFC plus it; with `efc_adc_debug` 1 it
prints "err= %d corr= %f efc= %f adc= %d".  The Z3815A's
`puck_efc_correct` (`0x3607c`) pairs with it at 0.450.

**Inferred.**  The Z3817A's oscillator module is the Z3815A's puck,
with its frames on a UART at 19200 where the Z3815A clocks them in on
port E.
