GPS Support Architecture

Three providers side by side, one engine. A second's record comes from a u-blox module on a microcontroller board, from a u-blox receiver through gpsd or a recording, or from a made-up sky; whichever fills it, the engine builds the same bytes. The crates are written once, in this repository; the board and smartclock-gps-feed on a host each run their own instance of them.

Notes

  • One copy, several instances. The board and the feed each make their own Engine from the same crate; neither talks to the other. The u-blox crate is the only UBX parser: the feed uses its Assembler, for gpsd or a recording, the board its framing, decoders and subframes under a provider of its own. The feed holds one provider at a time, column 2's or column 3's, and one engine.
  • Position hold. A SmartClock's normal running state: it holds its surveyed position, and its engine solves only for the clock. A survey is the exception. A u-blox receiver holding its position reports a fix of time alone (its fix type 5) and gives its position DOPs as placeholders, so the u-blox provider passes on no DOPs then, and the engine sends zeros for DOPs a record lacks.
  • Saved configuration. A VP keeps its settings and output rates through a power cycle, so an engine can be made already configured. CONFIGURATION_58503A is how a 58503A leaves its engine, for a receiver that cannot command its engine, as the Z3817A. @@Cf still returns it to the defaults.
  • 1 PPS. A record carries no pulse. On the board the u-blox module makes it and the board's provider shifts and gates it by the SmartClock's pulse settings; the host feed has none, and the instrument's comes from elsewhere.
  • Scope. The engines are the ones SmartClocks carry: the Oncore VP and UT Plus, and the Z3815A's Furuno GT-74. Each answers only what the receivers' firmware sends and fills in only what it decodes. smartclock-gps-feed runs both families; the board, in an Oncore socket, runs the Oncores.
